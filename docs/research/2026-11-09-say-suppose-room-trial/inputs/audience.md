# Say Suppose: the one viewer

Every seat reads the draft as this one person (owner, 2026-11-09: "one named viewer", `rules/story.md` and
`style.md`). The name is ours, so the room has someone to picture; the description is the owner's.

## Riya, 22, Pune

- **Who.** A young Indian, comfortable in English, watching on a phone in the evening: on the metro home, then in
  bed before sleep, sound on but low, one thumb on the screen.
- **Their week.** Chai at the stall outside college, the bus or the metro, a final-year exam season, a part-time
  internship in an office with a boss who messages late, and family at home: a mother who calls twice a day, a
  grandfather who tells the same story at every visit, a younger cousin. Every example and gag in a script comes
  from this week.
- **What they already daydream.** Pausing time before an exam, knowing what people really think of them, money that
  just arrives, one more evening with someone who is gone. The channel's what-ifs are daydreams they have had.
- **Packaging stays global.** They read English titles fine, and so does the rest of the world: no `₹`, no local
  slang in the English title or thumbnail. Inside the story, the chai and the metro stay.

## What makes them leave

From the first video's reviewers (five seats, rounds 1-5 and G4) and the owner's notes:

- **A lecture.** Two or three facts in a row while the story stands still ("It keeps stopping to teach me things";
  they would have left at 2:03 of the pause-time rough cut, on chronostasis).
- **Numbers right after a payoff.** Statistics straight after the rescue read as a road-safety ad.
- **A promise not kept.** The thumbnail's moment not shown in the first seconds.
- **An empty opening.** Seconds with nothing to hear or see before the first word (owner, 2026-11-08).
- **A stretch where nothing happens to you:** a mood, a montage that does not turn, a hold over a still picture.
- **Being told how to feel,** or a line that sounds like a teacher ("Anyone can look up from their phone at a
  crossing").

## What keeps them

- A person they care about, planted early, with warm beats before anything happens to them (the mother in the
  pause-time video).
- One line they would send a friend ("You're not a saint. You're a person with ten seconds").
- A plant they want to rewind for (the stranger hidden in the crowd).
- A choice put to them that the figure answers at once ("Promise her, or keep watching?").
