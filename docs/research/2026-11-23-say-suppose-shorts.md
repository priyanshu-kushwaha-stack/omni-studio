# Say Suppose Shorts: research notes (2026-11-23)

Research only. No repo files changed. No sign-in, no likes or comments.

## How the numbers were collected

- **Seen myself (S):** I loaded youtube.com signed out in the built-in browser on 2026-11-23 and read each video's
  own page data (`videoDetails.viewCount`, `lengthSeconds`, `publishDate`). These are exact public view counts on that
  day. Channel-list numbers are YouTube's rounded labels ("2.4 million views").
- **Frames (F):** for 13 Shorts I paused the player at about 0.3 s, a few seconds in, and the last second, and looked at
  the frame. I watched with the sound **muted**, so what I say about narration comes from the burned-in, word-by-word
  captions. I did not hear the audio.
- **Secondary (2nd):** a number taken from an article. The source is named next to it.
- Public view counts since 31 Mar 2025 (Shorts) and 24 Aug 2026 (every format) count every start and replay. They are
  higher than "engaged views", which the public can't see. See section 3.

## 2. What makes these Shorts get views

### 2a. The hook in the first 1–2 seconds

From the frames I viewed (F, 13 Shorts):

- **Second person, spoken and on screen, from frame 0.** Low Poly #3 "if you could stop time", #6 "if you could stop
  aging"; Zack D #9 "If you looked… You would see"; Prof. Gizzard #19 "YOU"; Kurzgesagt #16 "Can YOU evolve to never
  sleep?"; Lecuden #22 "What if you could pause time?". That is 6 of 9 animated what-if Shorts. 8 of the top 20 titles
  contain "you" or "your".
- **The premise is already happening in frame 0.** Rockets frozen mid-air (#3). The android already in the boot (#14).
  The POV already on the walkway into Saturn (#24). No logo, no intro, no "hey guys". The one long-form cut that opens
  on context instead (Primal Space #21, "In the mid 60s,") is a lower performer, though one example proves nothing.
- **Two title styles.** (a) No title card: the caption *is* the hook (Low Poly, Zack D). (b) A big question in the top
  third that stays or comes back (Kurzgesagt, What If, Lecuden, Awaken).
- Secondary: YouTube's Shorts analytics "How many chose to view" (viewed vs. swiped away) measures this decision.
  Creators cite 70%+ viewed as strong, but these are not YouTube benchmarks:
  https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/ ,
  https://gyre.pro/blog/youtube-shorts-view-count-update-impact-strategy-what-to-do-next

### 2b. Length

- **Our examples:** the 20 most-viewed Shorts in the table (#1–#20) run **30–78 s, median 60.5 s**. None is over 80 s.
  The 120–180 s cuts (#21, #22, #24, #25) sit at 0.7–1.4M.
- **The 3-minute ceiling** has applied to square or vertical uploads since 15 Oct 2024 (announced 3 Oct 2024):
  https://www.engadget.com/entertainment/youtube/youtube-shorts-can-now-run-up-to-three-minutes-160002081.html ,
  https://variety.com/2024/digital/news/youtube-shorts-maximum-video-length-three-minutes-1236166349/
- **Virlo (2nd)**, 10,827 Shorts posted 13–31 Oct 2026, views relative to channels of the same size: overall, under
  15 s scored 1.08×, 30–60 s 0.96×, 60–90 s 0.95×, 90 s–3 min 1.04×. For **channels under 100K, 60–90 s was the worst
  bucket (0.44–0.54×)**. For 100K–1M channels, 90 s–3 min was best (2.01×). Correlation only, and the sample leans
  toward Shorts that were already doing well:
  https://virlo.ai/data/how-long-should-a-youtube-short-be
- Other secondary figures (Metricool's 16 s average view duration; Inflow's 50–60 s from 2023) are summarised at
  https://riverside.com/blog/how-long-can-youtube-shorts-be and https://piktochart.com/blog/how-long-youtube-shorts/

### 2c. Looping

- (F) Low Poly #3 ends mid-word ("so you instant-") on a white flash, and #6 ends on a line that leads back into the
  opening. FactoHolic #1 ends on the same framing it opens on. Zack D #9 and #14 stop hard on the payoff image or word,
  with no outro, so the restart is instant.
- Since 31 Mar 2025 **every replay counts as a public view** (section 3), so a loop raises the public count directly.
  Engaged views exclude loops ("not including any loops", quoted in
  https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/shorts-views/ and similar glossaries; 2nd).
- Average percentage viewed can go above 100% when viewers rewatch (2nd, creator blogs, not YouTube):
  https://prepublish.ai/guides/youtube-shorts-retention . No controlled study of looping was found.

### 2d. On-screen text and burned-in captions

- **Style (F):** every animated hit I viewed burns in captions of 1–4 words at a time, centred horizontally, about
  65–78% of the way down the frame. That is above the bottom UI and below the action (Low Poly, Zack D, Kurzgesagt,
  Primal Space, What If, Prof. Gizzard). Two styles show up:
  - Small, plain captions that stay out of the way (Low Poly: thin yellow-white; Kurzgesagt: small white).
  - Big bold karaoke captions with a highlighted keyword (Awaken: white caps with a yellow keyword; Prof. Gizzard:
    one big word).
  - The question-title Shorts keep the title permanently in the top third, *below* the top UI (What If, Lecuden,
    Awaken).
- **UI-safe zones on 1080×1920:**
  - Google's single vertical-ad template, which also covers Shorts ads, is the strictest official guide. It keeps text
    out of the **top 288 px, bottom 672 px, left 48 px and right 192 px**, leaving a safe box from (48, 288) to
    (888, 1248). Google publishes no separate safe zone for organic Shorts. Pixel values via
    https://gummbleads.com/tools/youtube-shorts-safe-zone/ and
    https://www.socialync.io/tools/safe-zones/youtube-shorts , which cite Google Ads Help 9128498 and
    services.google.com/fh/files/misc/universalsafezones-youtube.pdf (2nd: I did not open the PDF myself).
  - Measurements of organic Shorts (2nd) are smaller. Top: about 120–380 px. Bottom: about 290–460 px (the
    channel/subscribe row starts about 290 px up; a two-line title pushes it higher). Right: about 120–200 px (the
    like/comment/share column):
    https://adchecklab.com/articles/youtube-shorts-safe-area-dimensions ,
    https://schedulenchill.com/blog/youtube-shorts-dimensions ,
    https://filmscribe.ai/en/tools/youtube-shorts-safe-zone
  - Since 25 Jun 2026 viewers can turn on a "clear screen" mode that hides all the overlays, but the default view still
    shows them: https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-shorts-experience-updates-features/
  - Practical box: keep captions between y ≈ 1150 and 1450 and x ≈ 60–880 at most, and titles between y ≈ 300 and
    600. That is consistent with where the hits place them (F) and passes the stricter measured zones. The ad template
    is stricter still at the bottom (y < 1248).

### 2e. Narration, music-only or trending audio

- Every animated hit I viewed (Kurzgesagt, Zack D, Low Poly, What If, Primal Space, Awaken, Prof. Gizzard, Lecuden) has
  word-by-word captions of spoken lines. That means **voice narration plus burned-in captions**, not music-only and
  not trending audio. Low Poly's description credits a composer, so it has a music bed too. (Inferred: I watched with
  the sound muted.)
- None of them uses a trending sound as the main track. I found no data comparing the three options for animated
  explainers.

### 2f. The payoff, and where it sits

- The payoff comes **at the very end, with no wind-down**. Zack D #14 lands its twist on the final word ("himself"), and
  #9 ends on the impact image.
- The other common ending is **a question to the viewer as the last frame**, which drives comments: Lecuden "WOULD YOU
  PRESS PAUSE?", Awaken "WHO'S YOUR +1?".
- The long-form cuts end on a presenter or CTA instead (What If #24) and score lower than the native ones. That is a
  pattern in our examples, not proof.

### 2g. Getting viewers from a Short to the long video

- **The Related-video link.** YouTube's own guide (blog.youtube, 14 Jul 2026) says the link can point to a long video,
  live stream or Short on the same channel; it is set in Studio → Content → the Short → Related video. YouTube advises
  a verbal **and** visual CTA in the last 5 seconds, such as pointing at the link or showing the long video's
  thumbnail, and that the long video should deliver on the Short's promise within its first 5–10 seconds. YouTube gave
  no click-through data:
  https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-related-videos-traffic-guide/
- **In practice (S):**
  - Low Poly Shorts #6 does exactly this: a "NEW LONGFORM WATCH NOW" thumbnail card in the last second plus the Rel.
    link. What If, Primal Space, Awaken, Veritasium and FactoHolic link to long videos.
  - Kurzgesagt links Shorts to *other Shorts*.
  - Zack D, Prof. Gizzard, Dr. Tie and Lecuden use no Rel. link.
- **How well it converts (no YouTube data exists):**
  - OutlierKit (2nd, Sep 2026): the link sends "a few hundred viewers per 100,000 Short views", about 0.3% or less:
    https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-to-long-form/
  - Subscribr (2nd, older): the link often converts under 1%; a pinned comment converts 1–3% (unverified):
    https://subscribr.ai/youtube-strategy/youtube-shorts-hurting-long-form-views
  - Market Maker (2nd, agency, unsourced): 2–4%. Treat as unverified:
    https://marketmakermgmt.com/blog-list2/how-shorts-and-long-form-work-together
  - Our own bound (S): Low Poly #6 (9.87M) links to a long video that has 209K views. Even if every one of those views
    came from the Short, that is ≤2.1%.
  - Awaken: the Short has 17K views and its long video has 5.38M, so the long video grew without the Short.
- **Pinned comments:** I did not check them. Comments load on demand and I did not open them.

## 3. Platform facts

- **How a Shorts view is counted:**
  - YouTube announced on 26 Mar 2025 that, **from 31 Mar 2025**, a Shorts view counts every time a Short starts to
    play or replays, with no minimum watch time. The old count was renamed **"engaged views"**; it appears in
    Analytics Advanced Mode and is what YouTube says to compare against older Shorts:
    https://www.searchenginejournal.com/youtube-changes-shorts-view-counts-no-change-to-monetization/543005/ ,
    https://ppc.land/youtube-changes-how-shorts-views-are-counted-from-march-31/
  - **24 Aug 2026:** the same first-frame view now applies to **every format** (long-form, live, podcasts). An engaged
    view is a click, or watching past the first few seconds. CTR, AVD and retention are still built on engaged views,
    and the recommendation algorithm did not change:
    https://blog.youtube/inside-youtube/engaged-views-youtube-explained/ (post dated 19 Aug 2026)
- **Shorts analytics:** the Content tab with the Shorts filter shows "Shown in feed" and "How many chose to view", the
  percentage of times viewers viewed versus swiped away. It counts Shorts-feed impressions only. YouTube publishes no
  benchmark. (2nd, quoting the YouTube help page):
  https://www.creatoressentials.com/glossary/viewed-vs-swiped-away/ ,
  https://1of10.com/blog/youtube-shorts-analytics/
- **Maximum length:** 3 minutes for square or vertical uploads since 15 Oct 2024 (see 2b). A 16:9 upload never becomes
  a Short. A 1080×1920 file with letterboxing does.
- **Monetisation and watch hours (YouTube Help, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851):**
  - "any qualified watch hours from Shorts views in the Shorts Feed won't count towards the 4,000 qualified public
    watch hours threshold."
  - Shorts qualify through a separate path: **10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days**, plus 1,000 subscribers. The
    other path is 4,000 public long-form watch hours in 12 months.
  - Shorts ad revenue and eligibility are based on engaged views, not the public count.
- **2025–2026 changes:**
  - **YPP from 1 Feb 2027** (blog.youtube, 10 Aug 2026):
    https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/
    - New applicants need 8,000 watch hours in 365 days or 20M Shorts views in 90 days.
    - Existing partners are not affected.
    - Shorts revenue sharing then needs 10M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days; channels below that keep their
      long-form earnings.
    - Partners must accept the new terms by 31 Jan 2027.
  - **Custom Shorts thumbnails** (announced 24–25 Jul 2026): Partner Program channels only, desktop only, no A/B
    testing (2nd): https://ppc.land/youtube-ends-2-year-wait-for-shorts-thumbnails-but-blocks-a-b-testing/
  - **25 Jun 2026:** dislike removed from Shorts, heart icon, playback speed, clear-screen mode:
    https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-shorts-experience-updates-features/
  - **1 Oct 2026, originality update:** Shorts recommendations favour original work and cut the reach of re-uploaded
    clips. Cutting your *own* long videos is not the target (2nd):
    https://support.google.com/youtube/blog/470890423/prioritizing-original-content-on-shorts?hl=en (I could not read
    the body), https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-shorts-original-content-update/ ,
    https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/418466/youtube-alters-shorts-algorithm-for-originality.html
  - Shorts series (23 Sep 2026) and a Shorts bonus pilot for invited creators (16 Oct 2026). Both 2nd via
    https://outlierkit.com/resources/youtube-algorithm-updates/

## 4. Reframing 16:9 animation for vertical

What I saw (F):

- **Rebuilt as native vertical:** Kurzgesagt, Zack D, Low Poly, Prof. Gizzard and Lecuden. The characters and action
  are staged for 9:16, with the title in the top third and the subject centred. These are the highest performers in
  the table. I could not confirm whether Kurzgesagt re-animates or re-crops; no source says.
- **Re-rendered or cropped 3D from a long video:** Primal Space #21 (Voyager centred in a vertical frame, archive
  footage cropped) and What If #24 (full-frame vertical POV). Both are 1–3 min cuts that link back to the long video:
  1.4M and 0.86M views.
- **Stacked / letterboxed:** What If #25 places 16:9 footage in the middle of a grey 9:16 field with the caption below
  the picture, inside a Short that otherwise runs full-frame: 706K views. Awaken #28 is flat 2D like ours, built
  vertically with a top title band and lower karaoke captions: 17K views. Its long video is the hit.
- **Performance evidence:** I found **no A/B data** comparing crop, re-stage and letterbox. Vendor guides say:
  - Letterboxing "looks broken" in the feed, and a 16:9 frame on a blurred fill uses only about a third of the screen.
  - A centre crop of 1920×1080 keeps 31.6% of the width.
  - "Rebuild rather than clip" when the idea, not the footage, carries the value.
