# Say Suppose: who taps, and in what mood

2026-11-13. The owner: "most of the videos we made so far are emotional sort of ... I want exciting stuff. What type of
audience would come and be more attracted to our type of content and what would be their mood like?" Data read from
public YouTube channel pages and comment sections on 2026-11-13, in the owner's Chrome; no paid tools.

## Method

For each comparable channel: the median views of its latest long uploads (Shorts and full-movie re-cuts left out), its
top videos by views, and each hit's multiple of that median (the outlier score from `2026-11-09-say-suppose-topic-scoring.md`).
Each title is tagged by what it promises: **power** (you can do something), **status** (you beat or rank others),
**reveal** (you see a hidden social truth), **spectacle** (danger, scale, disaster), **curiosity** (how something
works), **emotion** (loss, regret, a person to save). Then the top comments on four videos, to hear the mood.

Titles are a proxy for the promise, not the whole film: Ifverse's hits also carry a person to care about (reference
study, 2026-11-06). What the table shows is what people **tap for**.

## The comments: what they are like

Top comments by likes, Ifverse *Reaction Time 10x* (3,394 comments) and *500 IQ* (1,777), ConChaser *Future Wealth*:

- **They joke about themselves.** "If I were to have a brain 10x faster, then it would probably be average" (8.4K
  likes); "If u had 500 IQ, u would still find a way to procrastinate on homework" (2.1K).
- **They play the power like a game.** "My best option is to befriend them"; "I would try investing"; "you would sell
  the products anonymously".
- **They check the logic.** "So many flaws with the logic of this video" (365); "there is no way they wouldn't just
  let the guy retake the shot" (522).
- **Gamer, anime and meme literate:** ultra instinct, Geometry Dash frame-perfects, GTA 6, SCP, a portal gun.
- **They know it is AI-made and don't mind:** "bro spent all his claude weekly usage on this" (3.9K).
- **They ask for more powers:** "what if you could use 100% of your muscle strength".

The emotional film, *500 IQ to Save Your Sister* (472 comments): "Genuinely had me in tears at his mom's death", "this
should be its own movie", "why pay Netflix". Fewer people came, but the ones who did loved it, and it was a **sequel**:
they already knew Leo. Even there, the top comment is a joke.

## Who, and in what mood

Mostly teens and young adults on a phone (inferred from the references and the comment voice; we have no analytics of
our own yet). They arrive **bored and playful, wanting to feel powerful and clever**:

1. **"What would I do?"** The largest group. They insert themselves, scheme with the power and want to see it used
   smartly and bigger each time.
2. **Status.** Who is richer, smarter, taller, more wanted. Ranking people is the thrill.
3. **The nosy one.** Hidden social truths: crushes, who texts with AI, who wants you to fail.
4. **Spectacle and curiosity.** Doom, scale and "how does that even work".
5. **The feel-something viewer.** Real but small; they come back for characters they already know.

## What it means for us

- **People tap for excitement and stay for the ride; emotion is seasoning.** It deepens love among people already
  watching (and works best in a sequel), but it does not bring new viewers.
- **Our template inverted this.** The 2026-11-06 study copied Ifverse's emotional machinery (cost at 60%, the power
  against a person at 75-80%, 8-10 wordless seconds on the saddest beat), and `rules/story.md` #9 and #18 made a
  sacrifice peak a must-pass check. So every film ends on a loss.
- **The four AI premises fit the audience; the treatment is what needs to change.** Predicting every choice is
  status and scheming; renting your brain is spectacle and money; a year every night is the grind fantasy (Ifverse's
  *Kindergarten With All Your Memories*, 4x); seeing who uses AI to text you is the nosy cluster (crush, 4x).

## Proposed rule changes (for the owner to decide)

1. **The promise is the power working.** The thumbnail shows the power in use and someone stunned, never a sad face.
2. **The middle is set pieces of clever, escalating uses,** each a small win or a narrow escape, with the world
   reacting bigger each time (school, the city, the news, governments).
3. **Close the loopholes on screen.** The audience checks the logic: when a viewer would say "why not just invest?",
   the film says it first and answers it.
4. **A laugh in every scene.** Self-aware, the way the comments talk.
5. **Emotion is one short beat, at most, and never the ending.** Change `story.md` #18 so the running gag carries the
   peak, mind-boggling or triumphant, with no required "give something up"; make #9's person to lose optional, not the
   default. The end is a twist, a bigger consequence or a cliffhanger.
6. **Build for part two.** A winning power gets a sequel (500 IQ 1.4M, then Disappear 556K); a sequel is where an
   emotional film can work.
7. **Topic scoring:** the `feeling_map` example becomes thrill, triumph, laughter, awe, dread; the scorer marks a
   topic down when its best moment is a loss.
