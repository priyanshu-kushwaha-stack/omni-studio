# Claims ledger

Every number, date, name or quoted finding spoken in the script has one row here.
The script doctor may flag a missing claim; it never writes one.

Source of this ledger: Researcher run 2026-10-31 (24 rows). Every quote was copied from the record opened at the URL in the row, then checked by script against the saved text (whitespace-normalised). Nature, Springer, Wiley, Elsevier and the NCBI Bookshelf page were not read directly (Bookshelf returned a bot-check page), so papers were opened as: PubMed abstracts via NCBI E-utilities (`efetch db=pubmed`, same text as the PubMed page linked) and PMC full text via E-utilities (`efetch db=pmc`). The Xia et al. 2024 Nature paper is open access and was read in full on PMC (PMC10901737). The StatPearls "Coccyx Pain" chapter was read as its PubMed record (PMID 33085286), which carries the chapter's Anatomy section. The `note` column says when a quote is a secondary source describing a primary one.

**Read this first:**
- The gene story (c08-c14) is *one* 2024 paper, mostly mouse work. The paper says the Alu insertion "may have contributed" to tail loss. Never say "scientists found THE gene that deleted our tail" or "one mutation erased the tail".
- The "price" (c14) is the authors' *speculation*, built on mice. Say "the researchers suspect", never "losing our tail gave us spina bifida".
- "Why" (c22, c23) is unknown. The paper itself: "The specific evolutionary pressures ... are not clear". Never say "we lost our tails so we could walk upright" as fact. The fossil row c23 even cuts against it: the first tailless apes still walked on branches on all fours.
- Brief target "apoptosis" is **corrected** (c05): the one ultrastructural study opened found the tail's dying cells look *necrotic*, not apoptotic. Say "the cells die off and get cleared away".
- Brief target "Proconsul ~20 Mya" is **corrected** to "by at least about 18 million years ago" (c23).
- Brief target "coccyx = 3-5 fused vertebrae" is **corrected**: 3-5 vertebrae, but whether they are fused varies a lot (c15).
- Brief target "bears weight when sitting leaning back" is **dropped**: only "part of a tripod when seated" was sourced (c17).

## Contested claims (each becomes a spoken caveat)
- c05: do not say "apoptosis". The tail's cells die and are cleared (c04), mechanism wording stays generic.
- c08-c12: one 2024 study, mostly mice. "May have contributed", "a key piece", never "the gene that deleted our tail". Undoing it wouldn't bring a tail back (c12).
- c14: the neural-tube "price" is the authors' speculation from mice. "The researchers suspect it may have come with a price." Never "losing our tail causes spina bifida".
- c18, c20: true-tail counts depend on definitions; about half of tail-like lumps hide a spine problem (mixed true/pseudotails). No link to the TBXT Alu is sourced.
- c21: "longest reported to our knowledge": say "one of the longest".
- c22: why apes lost tails is unknown. "Probably" locomotion, by the authors' own hedge. Don't say "so we could walk upright".
- c23: fossil date is a secondary citation; say "about 18 million years ago". Tailless apes were still four-legged branch walkers then.
- Dropped from the brief: "sits on it when leaning back" (unsourced), "fused" as a rule (c15 says it varies), "~20 Mya Proconsul" (use c23).
