# What If Only 100 People Could Ever See You? — the first 60 seconds

Pilot script v1, 2026-11-27. Premise picked by the owner from the topic room (`backlog/topics-2026-11-27`,
`f-only-100-people-see-you`). Spec: `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-27-say-suppose-restart-pilot-design.md`.

This is the first minute of the film only: the cold open, the title question and the start of the rules (L4). The
same pictures serve both versions below; only the narration changes.

- **On-screen number (L5):** a glowing readout over your head, `99 / 100`. It's the list, and it moves only when the
  list changes.
- **World:** Shinjuku Station, Tokyo, at rush hour.
- **Voices (L3):** N = narrator; YOU and KENJI = short character lines.

## Version B: first person (the L3 variant)

Same pictures and timings.

> I'm standing in the middle of the busiest train station on Earth, shouting.
>
> YOU: HELLO?!
>
> About three and a half million people pass through here every day. Not one of them turns around.
>
> A man stops so close his rice ball brushes my nose. He checks the departures board. Straight through my face.
>
> He can't see me. Nobody here can. Except a hundred people. Exactly a hundred. I picked every one.
>
> Well. Ninety-nine. Grandma's number one. My best friend Kenji. A ramen cook who owes me a bowl. And one empty line.
>
> KENJI: You're late!
>
> A normal person knows about six hundred people by name. I get a hundred. For life. Let me explain.
>
> (title) So what if only a hundred people could ever see you?
>
> Here's how it works. One: if someone's not on my list, I don't exist to them. Not in person. Not on camera. Two: I can
> swap one name a day. Three: whoever I cut forgets I ever existed.

## What this minute sets up (L4)

- **Absurd detail:** the rice ball against your nose, the board read through your face.
- **The promised dilemma:** Grandma is slot 1, and the minute ends on her name.
- **The number that will move:** `99 / 100`. It hits `100 / 100` when line 100 is filled.
