| c01 | There's a law from 1878. Treasure found in the soil goes to the Collector. In writing. Into the government treasury. Keep quiet, and it's up to a year in prison. | https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/2286/1/A1878-06.pdf | Whenever any treasure exceeding in amount or value ten rupees is found, the finder shall, as soon as practicable, give to the Collector notice in writing— | sourced | The Indian Treasure-trove Act, 1878 (Act No. 6 of 1878, 13 February 1878), sections 3, 4 and 20, India Code PDF read through its Wayback copy (12 Jul 2025; India Code returned 403). Section 4 goes on: "and either deposit the treasure in the nearest Government treasury, or give the Collector such security as the Collector thinks fit". Section 20: "he shall, on conviction before a Magistrate, be punished with imprisonment for a term which may extend to one year, or with fine, or with both", and his share "shall vest in Government". "In the soil" is s. 3: "anything of any value hidden in the soil, or in anything affixed thereto". The ten-rupee threshold is not spoken (one number per line); if it is wanted, say "anything worth more than ten rupees". "Up to a year" is a maximum on conviction, never "a year". Maharashtra's amendment leaves ss. 4 and 20 as printed |
