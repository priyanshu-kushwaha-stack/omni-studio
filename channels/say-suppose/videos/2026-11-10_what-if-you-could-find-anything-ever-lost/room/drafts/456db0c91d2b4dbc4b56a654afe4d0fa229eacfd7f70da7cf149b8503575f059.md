# Packaging: find anything ever lost

status: draft for G1, 2026-11-10
topic: first /say-suppose-topics slate, g-find-anything-ever-lost, ranked 1st at 83/100, proposed by Gemini 4 Argon

## Premise

You pat your pockets three times before every metro door, and nothing of yours stays lost. Tonight the third pat finds
no earbud, you mutter "where did it go", and a glowing dotted line draws itself from your shoe into the drain grate.
The line is invented. The real-world part, to source at G2, is what happens to a found thing: who owns it, who gets to
dig, and how much of the world's lost fortune nobody will ever reach.

## The viewer

Riya (`audience.md`), 22, Pune, final-year exams, the metro home with sound low, a part-time internship, a mother who
calls twice a day and a grandfather who tells the same story at every visit. Here "you" are the person in that house
who loses nothing, in the week the grandfather's story turns out to be wrong. Every gag comes from the week: the metro
gap, the courtyard drain, the Sunday lunch, the cousin with a phone. The English packaging stays readable worldwide:
no rupee sign, no local slang.

## Five lines (rules/story.md, check 1)

> SITUATION: You pat your pockets three times at every metro door; nothing of yours stays lost, and your grandfather tells the same story every Sunday about the brother who took the orchard deed.
> DESIRE: To find everything: your earbud, your mother's earring, then the fortunes nobody is coming back for.
> CONFLICT: Every line you follow ends in someone's hands: a law, a landfill, a sea, and then your own courtyard.
> CHANGE: The deed your grandfather says his brother stole was hidden by his late wife under the tulsi he waters every morning, and the line takes you straight to it.
> RESULT: You step off the metro without patting your pockets, nudge a stranger's dropped key with your toe, and leave the rest where it lies.
> UNDER: If I could find anything, is there something I should leave lost?

No new power rule after the halfway point; no new person after 60% (the great-uncle is on screen at about 2:20 with
a mango). Never say the feeling (check 11): the script shows the crooked tulsi and the handwriting; it does not name
the shame. Every find in the first jump is small, personal and funny; the narration from the dump onward is cinematic,
never a documentary voice over a fact.

**Rule device:** the teal dotted line from your shoes, with a stamped distance tag that counts down as you walk; it
snaps off and redraws when you whisper for something new, and nothing appears at all when someone alive knows.

**Research for G2** (from the brief's content mine; nothing here is a claim until `claims.md` cites it, all of it
not verified yet): the Indian Treasure Trove Act of 1878 (the ten-rupee threshold, the Collector, the one-year term:
check the Act's own text, not a summary); Armory v Delamirie, 1722 (the finder against everyone except the owner, and
how modern law treats mislaid things); the nyariya gold-washers of bazaar drains (the World Gold Council link in the
brief covers household gold only; the drain-washers need their own source); Chainalysis's estimate of about 3.7 million
bitcoin lost, "nearly 20%" (an estimate, spoken as one); James Howells and the Newport landfill (since 2013, 8,000 BTC,
110,000+ tonnes: the figures move by year, use the source's); UNESCO's "over 3 million" wrecks; the Broken Arrows list
(32 accidents, 6 weapons never recovered, Tybee Island 1958). Carried from Opus's notes: the Zeigarnik "twice as much"
figure is overstated and no beat rests on it; the Schacter line on absent-mindedness is optional and goes in only if
it fits in one sentence at the opening. Dropped with their beats: the RBI unclaimed deposits, the Gardner heist, and
Craven's Bayesian search.

**The viewer's "but what about"** (answered where they arise): Why the earbud and not the bicycle? Can I just keep
what I find? Why not go for the big money, the bitcoin and the wrecks? If the person who hid it is dead, does it count
as lost? Why not find everything, all the time? Left open for the comments, on purpose: what about a thing that was
stolen, and then the thief died?

**Past 8 minutes, why a viewer stays:** at 8:00 the envelope is on your desk, read by you and not by him, and his
brother is walking to the gate; the viewer stays to see his face when he reads her handwriting.
