## What the sources say

- **The Tail End (c01).** Tim Urban (Wait But Why, December 2015) counts his remaining time with his parents. During
  his first 18 years he saw them on at least 90% of days; since moving out, about five visits a year each, about two
  days each, "10 days a year". With his parents in their mid-60s and an optimistic 30 more years, "that's 300 days
  left", "less time than I spent with them in any one of my 18 childhood years", and "when I graduated from high
  school, I had already used up 93% of my in-person parent time". It is a worked illustration on his own
  assumptions, so the script says "on his own guesses" and does its own sum for you from the story's counter.
- **Lasts taste better (c02).** O'Brien and Ellsworth (Psychological Science, 2012): 52 students tasted five
  chocolates in random order. Told "This is the last one" before the fifth, 64% chose it as their favourite, against
  22% when it was just "the next one". The "last" group also enjoyed the whole tasting more. It is a small,
  everyday study, and the script uses it for a small, everyday moment (the station chai).
- **The country goes home (c03).** Press Information Bureau, October 2025: more than 12,000 special trains over the
  61 days from 1 October to 30 November 2025 for Puja, Diwali and Chhath, up from 7,724 the year before; 1,500
  specials over five days at the Chhath peak. The minister announced the 12,000 in August 2025.
- **Short time, close people (c04).** Fung, Carstensen and Lutz (Psychology and Aging, 1999): older people prefer
  familiar social partners and younger people do not, but when time is framed as short, both ages do. In Hong Kong,
  two months before the 1997 handover, both age groups preferred familiar partners; a year before and a year after,
  only older people did. Socioemotional selectivity theory.
