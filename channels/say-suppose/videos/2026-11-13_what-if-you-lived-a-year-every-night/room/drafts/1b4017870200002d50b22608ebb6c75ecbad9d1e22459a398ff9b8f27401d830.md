# Packaging: a year every night

status: G1 draft, round 1 (2026-11-13). Written by Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude -p`)

## Premise

At bedtime you plug a cable into your sleep pod, and your mind runs about a thousand times faster until the alarm. Eight hours out here is a year in there: a town of other sleepers, a job, four seasons, friends, weather. You remember all of it. Your body ages one night.

**The catch.** Once the year starts it runs to the end, and nobody out here comes with you. The people in the flat above you keep their own speed, so every morning you come back a year older in your head to someone who has had one night. What you bring back is only what is in your head, and a head holds the new by rubbing out the old: a year of new, every night, and last night out here never stood a chance. The more you live in there, the less of her you have left.

**Why this setting.** Osaka, a hotel of sleep pods where you and your flatmate work the night desk, and sleep in the staff pods after close. The first hotel of pods in the world opened in Osaka in 1979 (c01), so the world needs no explaining: rows of people lying side by side in two-metre boxes, room to sit up but not stand (c02), is already a picture of people plugged in next to each other, and the only new thing in the frame is a cable. The hotel also gives the film its engine without a new rule: the boss owns the pods, the boss sees money, and the city plugs in through this one door, so every wild use of the power (the exam student, the judge, the bride, the morning train) walks past your desk. I weighed an overnight sleeper train, whose bunks are already in the set library, but a train arrives somewhere each morning and this story needs the same door and the same flatmate at the same speed every single day. I weighed a hospital ward of beds in rows, but illness colours the idea, and a row of people lying under blankets is tonight's other film. This one is different on purpose: no bedroom, no blanket, no headband, no broker. You sit up and climb out of a hatch in a dark hall of pods; the flat upstairs is a kitchen with a kettle, never a bed. Nothing in the telling needs a local word: "sleep pod", "a hotel of pods", "the night desk", "your flatmate", "your boss", and the one real-world name spoken is the city's.

**Invented:** you, your flatmate, your boss, the hotel's staff and guests, the cable and the machine, the thousand-times speed as a thing that exists, the inside town and its seasons, the day counter on the hatch, every night count and year count in the story, the old sleeper, the slow room and its queue, the exam board, the judge, the bride, the kid in the cap, the two-plug cable. **Real:** the first hotel of pods, Osaka, 1979 (c01); a pod about two metres by one, sit up but not stand (c02); a year is 8,760 hours (c03); Robin Hanson's guess that an uploaded mind would typically run about a thousand times human speed, more than three years a day (c07); the sleeping brain replaying a learned route six to seven times faster, in rats (c10); dreams running at about real speed, squats about 40% slower (c11); one small famous 1924 experiment, more than twice as many nonsense words kept after sleep, forgetting as the new rubbing out the old (c13); sleep as when the brain files the day (c14); Michel Siffre, about two months in a cave in 1962, 25 days lost (c16); people dropped 31 metres later judging their own fall 36% longer, a moment as long as the memory it leaves (c18); Sergei Krikalev, 803 days in orbit, a forty-eighth of a second younger (c22); about 50, 90 and more than 200 hours to make a casual friend, a friend, a close friend (c23); 2,200 class hours for Japanese, Mandarin, Arabic or Korean at a working level (c26); Jeanne Calment, 122 years and 164 days (c27); the English of Beowulf unreadable today without study (c28).

## Five lines (rules/story.md, check 1)

> SITUATION: Osaka, a hotel of sleep pods where you and your flatmate work the night desk. Everything she tells you, you answer "tell me tomorrow", and your boss has just unpacked a cable that makes tomorrow a year away.
> DESIRE: To come back every morning with more to give her.
> CONFLICT: Each year inside buys you skills, friends and a whole life, and costs you one more night of hers, until she is someone you see for a few hours a decade.
> CHANGE: You stop counting what the cable gives you and start counting whose minutes you are in.
> RESULT: You hang the cable on its hook, and when she starts the story again, you say "Tell me now."
> UNDER: If I had a thousand lifetimes, who would I still make time for?
> DARLING: "Yesterday was a year ago."
> DARLING: "Out there a close friend takes more than 200 hours (c23). In here, that's twelve minutes of her evening."
> DARLING: "The oldest minds in the world are queueing for a room where a minute costs a minute."

**How it differs from the nearest video.** Ifverse's "One Second Felt Like Ten" slows the world for one body across a school day; the power is a reflex and the stakes are a corridor. Ours is a nightly round trip with a fixed exchange rate, a year for eight hours, and the story is the arithmetic of a friendship: a flatmate who keeps one speed while you spend ninety-nine hours in a hundred somewhere she can't follow. Tonight's other film, the AI renting your brain while you sleep, is a bedroom, a blanket, a headband and a broker, and someone else uses your hours; here you use them, all of them, in a dark hall of pods, and the cost is that you have too many.
