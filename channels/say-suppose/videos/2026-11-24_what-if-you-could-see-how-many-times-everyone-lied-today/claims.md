# Claims ledger: What If You Could See How Many Times Everyone Lied Today? (G1)

Started at G1 on 2026-11-24. A research subagent fetched every page below on 2026-11-24 (read-only: WebSearch, WebFetch, curl; no sign-ins, no paid APIs). Quotes are copied exactly from the fetched page or record. Most publisher pages (Wiley, SAGE, Taylor & Francis, APA, ResearchGate, Academia) refused the fetch (403 or connection refused), so abstracts were read from the PubMed efetch record (PMID given) or, where a paper is not in PubMed, from the OpenAlex API record for the DOI, which mirrors the publisher abstract; those rows say so. Full-text quotes come from open copies named in each note. Where a figure is not in an abstract, the note says which citing page or press release gave it.

The story is invented and carries no row: the counter and its rule, you, every person and every number inside the story.

**Corrections made in research** (never say the left side):
- "You hear 10 to 200 lies a day" (Meyer, TED 2011) -> the national survey found the average American reported about 1.65 lies in the past 24 hours, and about 60% reported none; about half of all lies came from about 5% of people (c03, c04, c12).
- "Everybody lies all day" -> most people lie rarely; a few prolific liars tell most lies (c15, c17).
- "Strangers lie three times in ten minutes" -> in one experiment, about 60% lied at least once in a 10-minute chat, averaging two to three lies; the people were told to seem likable or competent in some conditions, so it is not a typical-chat rate (c05).
- "81% of online daters lie" needs its size: the lies were mostly small, e.g. under an inch of height on average (c07).
- "Your nose heats up when you lie" -> the 2012 Granada press release said the nose got warmer; a 2017 journal paper reported the nose temperature tended to decrease. Do not state a direction (c13, c18).
- "Polygraphs do not work" -> the report says little basis for extremely high accuracy, while also saying specific-incident tests do better than chance. Do not say they are useless (c09).
- "Lies get bigger because of the brain" -> one lab task (guessing pennies in a jar for gain), co-authored by Dan Ariely; do not generalise (c10).
