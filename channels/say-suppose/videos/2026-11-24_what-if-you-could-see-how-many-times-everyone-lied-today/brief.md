## The premise (yours to improve)

One morning a small glowing counter floats over every person's head: how many lies they have told since midnight. It
ticks +1, with a soft click, the instant a lie leaves their mouth. It resets at midnight. Only you can see it. Your own
counter you cannot see. That is the catch to build on, and it pays off late.

## What the channel analysis found (this film's targets)

Our three public films got 955, 30 and 23 thumbnail impressions. Pause-time had a 2.9% click rate, and 43% of viewers
were gone by 0:32. Six new "What If You…" cartoon channels got 1.7M to 5.4M views within five uploads. What they do
that we didn't:

1. **Thumbnail = one big reacting face + one glowing number that IS the premise** (ConChaser: "$150" over you and
   "$8,742,619,300" over a homeless man, no words; Ifinite: "52 YEARS" and "00:00:05" over figures). Our tags needed
   the film to make sense ("DAY 12", "THAT'S ME?", "NOT HIM."). For this film: a person grinning at you mid-sentence,
   with a big glowing counter over their head, and you large at the left, stunned. Dark set; the counter's glow is the
   light. A tag, if any, at most three words, and it must make sense without the film. One of the three candidates may
   carry no tag at all (the number alone), since K5 allows zero words; say which.
2. **The first 30 seconds show the power working.** The first shot is the thumbnail's shot, with its number on screen
   by 0:05. The power is in use before any backstory. The title question comes by 0:20. No slow set-up, no hardship
   backstory before the first win.
3. **A running number on screen.** Every head carries its counter all film long; add one corner readout that climbs as
   the world widens (for example, the lies told today in the room, then the city, then on live TV).
4. **Length 9 to 10:30 at about 155 words a minute**, about 1,300 to 1,450 spoken words in all. (The local narrator,
   voice B, reads nearer 180 a minute, so keep the count honest.) Our last two films ran 12:37 and 13:12.
5. **Told at scale, in a world anyone knows.** Escalate from one person to a room, to the city, to the whole world
   (a live televised event, a stock price, a world leader at a podium). Open somewhere every viewer knows (a kitchen
   table, a school, a party, a first date), not a local institution that needs explaining. The country is never needed.
6. **Exciting, not sad** (taste.md, 2026-11-13): the power used cleverly and bigger each time, the world reacting, a
   laugh in every scene, the loopholes closed on screen. One short tender beat at most, never the peak, never the end.

## Real facts the film can stand on (claims.md has the rows and exact wording)

The research ledger `claims.md` (provided below) holds the sourced facts. The strongest turn the research offers: the
popular belief that people hear "10 to 200 lies a day" is wrong. In large studies most people tell no lies at all on a
given day, and about half of all lies come from a few prolific liars. So when the counters switch on, most heads read 0
or 1, and a few glow in the dozens. Use that as the film's wrong belief and its turn if it serves the story. Speak only
what a row supports, at its precision; cite rows inline as (cNN) in the beat outline.
