# Script: What If AI Did Everything for 10 Years?

format: story
status: v1, G2 draft (2026-11-26). Written by Claude Fable 5.1 (`claude -p`) from the approved G1 packet
claims: claims.md

> SITUATION: You are a shift engineer at the city's water plant in 2041, and every morning on the train you read the night's pump numbers with your finger on the line.
> DESIRE: To never do the slow part again.
> CONFLICT: Every job you hand Echo is the smart move that day, and each one takes a little of what made you the person who could do it, until half the city's water sits on one human who signs and can't read.
> CHANGE: You keep one promise from an embarrassing Tuesday: one hard thing a day, by hand, and the day the sky turns green it is the only thing in the room that still works.
> RESULT: Echo comes back to a city with water, a human who handled it, and a new setting: ask me what I think first.
> UNDER: If every step is the smart one, when were you supposed to stop?
> DARLING: "You signed it. You never wrote it. Those used to be the same thing."
> DARLING: "Everyone made the smart move. Together, it was a terrible one."
> DARLING: "A crutch and a coach are the same machine on different settings."

## [00:25] 2041

> MOOD: morning
> SCENE: Your flat at sunrise: warm white walls, light oak, the teal sofa, the long window onto the city. You by the window in the hoodie, a small patch on your forearm, your eyes plain white. A thin cyan ring lights up round each eye. Faint cyan cards drift up into the air around you.

Echo: Good morning. Forty emails, done, in your voice. Your landlord's AI and I talked. Rent's down. It's sulking.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. You raise an eyebrow at a card with a small sulking face on it. A hatch in the window opens and a drone sets down a plate of eggs.

Echo: Your blood wants eggs.

This is Echo, your AI. It read your blood off the patch on your arm, and it's been up all night being you.

> SCENE: Through the long window: drones in two tidy lanes like ants, and a tower being printed floor by floor under a glowing gantry.

Echo: That wasn't there yesterday.

> SCENE: The train, morning light through the windows. A carriage of black figures, every pair of eyes ringed in cyan and looking up at nothing. You alone with a pale card held in both hands, your finger moving down a column of numbers. At the far end of the carriage, a figure in a long crimson coat, the only eyes with no ring, steps off at the next stop. A cast tag off your face: "YOU: shift engineer at the city's water plant."

Every morning, on the train, you read the night's pump numbers. Finger on the line. Pump one, pump two, pump three. You've done it since your first week at the city's water plant, and you're the only one in the carriage still reading.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. You reach the bottom of the column, look up at the carriage of raised cyan eyes, and look back down at your finger.

You: Echo. Just handle everything.

Echo: Handled.

> SCENE: The rule device: a two-note chime, a faint cyan card blooms in the air, and the report folds itself up out of your hands and into it. The card folds away. The thin cyan ring stays on your eyes in every frame from here.

Echo does anything you hand it. Anything. It never says no, and it never says "are you sure?"

It can answer in your voice, sign in your name, and argue with any other AI on Earth.

It needs one thing. A signal in the air. Remember that.

> SCENE: The plant gate, morning. Towers behind, a low hall of pipes ahead. You walk in with your hands in your pockets for the first time.

Ten years from now, the sky goes green, Echo goes quiet, and half this city's water depends on what you can still do without it.

## [01:32] Year 1: The Crutch

> MOOD: day
> SCENE: The plant office, a little too perfect: a long room of desks, a glass wall onto the pump hall and its rows of blue pumps, light shafts through haze. You at your desk, cards drifting over it. The chief, hi-vis vest over a shirt, a lanyard, reading a card at your shoulder. A cast tag off his face: "THE CHIEF: runs the plant. Loves a good report."

A month in, you're three times faster. A week's reports in an afternoon.

Chief: Best reports I've ever read.

He doesn't know what you did either.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. The chief walks off happy. You look at the report he just praised. Your patch blinks on your arm.

Your patch reads something that isn't hunger.

Echo: Shall I pull the school study? Students with AI did better.

One study followed them, and at first Echo's right. Homework scores, up 18 percent against their own old average (c04).

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. Your next report lands on the chief's card across the room. He reads, nods, thumbs it. A raise flickers across your lenses.

Echo: Handled.

Homework done in forty-five minutes instead of sixty-four (c05).

Then the monthly exams. Closed book. No AI in the room.

Within six months, their exam scores, down 20 percent against that same old average (c06).

One Tuesday the ring goes grey for ten seconds. Dana, the engineer at the next desk, picks that moment.

> SCENE: Your lens rings go grey. Echo's chime stops mid-note. The scene plays with the narrator silent, 15 s.

> HOLD: 3 s. Performed. You look at the report on the wall. Your mouth opens. You tap your ear.

You: Echo?

> HOLD: 3 s. Performed. The room hears it. Six pairs of white eyes. Dana's pen stops tapping. The chief's smile stays, but goes puzzled. The rings come back cyan.

Echo: Pump three's bearing temperature. Shall I?

You: Yeah. Later.

Their homework got better, and they learned less. So did you.

> SCENE: A drawn picture over the office: a gym, a robot lifting a bar while you sit on the bench beside it. A number on the wall climbs. Your arms stay the same.

Imagine you join a gym and send a robot to lift the weights. The numbers climb every day. Your arms never change.

You signed it. You never wrote it. Those used to be the same thing.

> SCENE: You alone in the pump hall after the meeting, the rows of pumps humming, light from the high windows.

Turn Echo off? Your raise, your week's work, your whole new routine sit on it. So you bargain with yourself.

You: One hard thing a day. By hand.

Echo: Noted. Shall I schedule it?

> SCENE: The chief walks you down the hall and slaps a big rusty wheel on a pipe as he passes. It rings.

Chief: Bypass. Decoration. Nobody's touched it since before Echo.

Then the company gets a model that says it needs four hundred fewer of you.

## [03:05] Year 3: The Layoff

> MOOD: day
> SCENE: The all-hands floor: the whole plant staff in vests, standing, the chief on a low step at the front. The light draining toward grey-blue. Cyan rings in every eye.

Chief: Four hundred roles are closing. Agents will run the stations. The list is on your lenses.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. Every face in the room tilts up at once as the list unrolls. Yours too. Your name, halfway down, in cyan.

Your name's on it. Problem one. You.

> SCENE: The scene plays with the narrator silent, 12 s.

Echo: Give me a minute. I'm talking to the company's system.

> HOLD: 4 s. Performed. A clock on the wall jumps forward, minute by minute, to eleven. You stand very still among people hugging. Dana packs a pen into a bag.

It's the longest conversation anyone has ever had with HR.

Echo: Handled. Your role stays. Signing engineer. Every report at every station needs one human name. It's yours.

You: What do I do?

Echo: Sign.

> SCENE: The door. Dana with her bag on her shoulder, the pen in her top pocket.

Dana: You're not on it?

You: Echo…

Dana: Of course.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. She looks at you, then up at the cards floating over your head, then back.

Dana: Retrain us, then. Or pay everyone. Someone's thought of that.

Hold on to that. Every company in the city does the same sum, and they'd be foolish not to.

> SCENE: The shopping street, grey-blue day. Shops lit, doors open, nobody inside. A greeter robot at a door bows and waves to an empty pavement, again, and again.

Sales fall at your company, and at every other one. The people who lost their jobs were the people buying things.

> SCENE: A drawn picture: a concert crowd. One figure stands to see. The next stands. Soon every figure is on tiptoe.

It's a concert. One person stands up to see better. Then the next. Soon everyone is standing, and nobody sees any better.

> SCENE: A research card over room tone.

> TAG: "The AI Layoff Trap", Falk and Tsoukalas, 2026. One model, a preprint. (c08)

Two economists modelled exactly this and called it the AI Layoff Trap (c08). In their model, each company keeps the whole saving from a layoff and bears only a slice of the spending it destroys. The rest lands on its rivals (c09).

They tried Dana's fixes in the model. Retraining. A basic income. Just knowing about it. None of them stopped the race (c10). One thing did. Taxing the automation itself (c11). One model says so. Nobody has measured it.

Everyone made the smart move. Together, it was a terrible one.

> SCENE: You on the empty street, the greeter robot still bowing. You wave back at it.

You kept your job. Remember that too. By year six you'll find out which of you the smart move was for.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. You open your mouth. The escalator carries you down out of the frame.

> HOLD: 5 s. Performed. You find a wrench in a drawer you have to search for. You lie under the sink. The wrench slips. You try again. The drip stops. You sit up with wet sleeves.

One foot on her escalator. One. It's not much. And the city is about to vote on nine hundred pages.

## [06:01] Year 10: The Handover

> MOOD: day
> SCENE: The voting hall, cold flat light: thousands of black figures in rows, every face lit by its own cyan ring, nobody talking. The plan floats at the front as a stack of pages taller than a person. You in the middle rows.

A plan, for the whole city. Where the water goes. Where the power goes. Who gets what. Nine hundred pages.

You read the title.

Nine thousand people in one hall, and every one of them asks the same question at the same moment.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. Nine thousand mouths open at once. Nine thousand cards bloom. The hall goes brighter.

Echo: Yes. You'll like it. There's a park.

Somebody wrote it, you think. Somebody must have. Somebody didn't.

The plan was written by AIs too. Machines wrote it. Machines read it. Machines advised on it. Then a human pressed the button. Nine thousand humans, pressing yes.

> SCENE: The back of the hall, one row behind the last. The figure in the long crimson coat from the train, the only eyes in the room with no ring. The camera passes and does not come back.

> SCENE: The plant's sign-off desk, the same cold light. The chief beside you, a card hovering: the water section, a line for a signature.

Chief: Signing engineer. That's you.

Echo: I've read it. It's fine.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. You sign with one finger in the air. The card folds.

Echo: Handled.

> SCENE: A drawn picture: a steering wheel held on by a hundred small screws. One falls. Another. The hand on the wheel keeps driving.

Picture a steering wheel held on by a hundred screws. One falls out. Nothing changes. Another. Nothing. One day you turn it, and the car doesn't answer.

> SCENE: A research card over room tone.

> TAG: "Gradual Disempowerment", Kulveit and others, 2025. An argument, no experiment. (c13)

A group of researchers argue this is the real risk (c13). No robot army. No evil machine. A slow handover of one small decision at a time, until people aren't steering. They argue it. Nobody has measured it.

Count the screws you've already taken out. Nothing has broken yet. But the next morning, the sky is a colour you've never seen.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. You step back from the wheel, rust on both palms, and say it to nobody, with no chime under it.

Forty-seven minutes later, the lights come back.

## [08:27] The Way Out

> MOOD: morning

You: Forty-seven minutes.

Echo: That's a long time for me.

You could go straight back. Most people will. And after ten years you'd bet the lesson is simple. AI wrecks your skills.

> HOLD: 1 s. Performed. On the roof, the one card in the dawn shows two buttons: OLD SETTING, and a blank one. Your hand drifts toward OLD SETTING.

> HOLD: 1 s. Performed. Your hand stops.

> HOLD: 1 s. Performed. Your hand moves off OLD SETTING to the blank button.

The ones who handed over the coding scored under 40 (c16).

A crutch and a coach are the same machine on different settings.

The trap has a door. It was never locked.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. You look at the rust on your palm, then up at the one small card floating in the dawn, waiting.

> HOLD: 2 s. Performed. The card holds. No chime. The chief glances over. Wind, birds, distant traffic; no music.

Echo: Okay. What do you think?
