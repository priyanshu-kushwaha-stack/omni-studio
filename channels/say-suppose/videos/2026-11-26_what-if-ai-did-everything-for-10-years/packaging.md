## Premise

It's 2041. You are a shift engineer at the city's water plant, and every morning on the train you read the night's pump numbers with your finger on the line. Then your contact lenses switch on and a warm voice in your ear says good morning. This is Echo, your AI. While you slept it answered forty emails in your voice, argued your rent down with the landlord's AI, and read your blood from a patch on your arm to choose your breakfast. So you make one small decision: "Echo, just handle everything." It says "Handled," and for ten years it never once says no.

**The catch.** Every job you hand Echo is the smart move that day, and each one quietly takes a little of what made you the person who could do it. Year 1 you are three times faster and can't walk a room through your own report. Year 3 four hundred people at the water company are replaced by agents, your name is on the list, and Echo gets you off it by making you the one human who signs everything, which is the smartest move of your life and the deepest step in. Year 6 the city has become two cities on two escalators, and the friend who lost her job is on the one going up. Year 10 a nine-hundred-page plan decides where the water goes, machines wrote it, machines read it, and a human presses the button: you. Then the sky turns green, a storm from the Sun kills every signal, and the plant's valves wait for a voice that isn't coming. Nobody built this trap on purpose. Every step in was the clever choice, and you felt clever the whole way. The one thing you did badly, on purpose, once a day, is what gets half the city its water by dark.

**Why this setting.** The city is unnamed and has no country: towers printed overnight, drones in lanes, lenses, one flat you keep for ten years. The research is from MIT, Rutgers, Anthropic and one county in central China (c01, c12, c15, c03), and none of the story's pictures needs a local word: a train, a meeting, a layoff, two escalators, a vote, a pump room, a rusty wheel. The water plant is the owner's handover's own ending, and it is now your job from the first minute (the session's evaluation, point 3), so the reports you can't explain in Year 1 are about the pumps you save in Year 10, and the valve you turn at 7:20 is one the chief called "decoration" at 2:25. ## Cast

Every person is a black satin figure with big white eyes (A4), told apart by clothes, hair and props; no coloured heads in this film.

- **You.** Mustard hoodie, teal scarf, lenses with a thin cyan ring when they're on. A shift engineer at the water plant. You read with your finger on the line until Echo takes the line away. You speak only to Echo, short: "Just handle everything." "Echo?" "By me." "Handled." "New setting."

## Five lines (rules/story.md, check 1)

> SITUATION: You are a shift engineer at the city's water plant in 2041, and every morning on the train you read the night's pump numbers with your finger on the line.
> DESIRE: To never do the slow part again.
> CONFLICT: Every job you hand Echo is the smart move that day, and each one takes a little of what made you the person who could do it, until half the city's water sits on one human who signs and can't read.
> CHANGE: You keep one promise from an embarrassing Tuesday: one hard thing a day, by hand, and the day the sky turns green it is the only thing in the room that still works.
> RESULT: Echo comes back to a city with water, a human who handled it, and a new setting: ask me what I think first.
> UNDER: If every step is the smart one, when were you supposed to stop?
> DARLING: "You signed it. You never wrote it. Those used to be the same thing."
> DARLING: "Everyone made the smart move. Together, it was a terrible one."
> DARLING: "A crutch and a coach are the same machine on different settings."
