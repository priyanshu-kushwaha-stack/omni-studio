# Handover: "What If AI Did Everything for 10 Years?"

Channel: Say Suppose. Prepared 2026-11-26. This file is self-contained: packaging, research, the full script, and the production direction.

## 1. What this video is

A 9-minute narrated "what if" film. The viewer ("you") lives through ten years of letting an AI assistant handle everything, and each stage of the story is one "AI trap" described in a real research paper.

- **Audience:** 10 to 40 years old, smart, has never read an economics paper.
- **Voice:** every idea explained as you would to a five-year-old, with one everyday picture per idea and no jargon. Second person, present tense.
- **Tone:** tense and cinematic, not doom. The AI is never evil and its voice never turns cold.
- **Big idea:** nobody builds the trap on purpose. Every step into it is the smart choice for the person taking it.
- **Length:** about 1,350 words of narration, roughly 9 minutes at a calm pace.

## 2. Packaging

- **Title:** What If AI Did Everything for 10 Years?
- **Backup title:** What If You Let AI Run Your Life?

## 3. Research behind each act

| Act | Trap | Paper | Plain-words claim | Type of evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | The Crutch Trap | Caosun and Aral (MIT), "The Augmentation Trap", 2026 | The practice you skip is what made you good. Lean on AI too much and you can end up worse off than with no AI. | Economic model |
| Year 1 | The Homework Trap | Strömberg, Lei and Wu, "The Generative AI Learning Penalty", 2026 | 26,811 students: homework scores up 18%, homework time down 30%, exam scores down 20% within six months. | Measured data |
| Year 3 | The Layoff Trap | Falk and Tsoukalas, "The AI Layoff Trap", 2026 | Each company keeps the savings from a layoff, but the lost customers are shared by everyone. Only a tax on automation stops the race. | Economic model |
| Year 6 | The Split | Zhifeng Cai, "The AI Skill Trap", 2026 | Above a skill line people keep climbing; below it they slide into full dependence. | Economic model |
| Year 10 | The Slow Handover | Kulveit and others, "Gradual Disempowerment", 2025 | No robot war. Humans hand over one small decision at a time until they are not steering. | Position paper (an argument) |
| Blackout | The Quiet Brain | MIT Media Lab, "Your Brain on ChatGPT", 2025 | 54 people wrote essays; the AI group struggled to quote what they had just written. | Small study, not peer-reviewed when reported |
| Way out | Coach or crutch | Shen and Tamkin (Anthropic), "How AI Impacts Skill Formation", 2026 | 52 developers: about 50% on a quiz with AI, about 65% without. Those who asked the AI to explain kept learning. | Small experiment, preprint |

### Rules for using the research

- Put the paper's name and year on screen whenever a number is spoken.
- Say "one model predicts" for the three economic models and "one small study found" for the MIT study.
- The story world is fiction. Only the on-screen cards carry real findings.
- **Not yet verified:** every figure above came from abstracts and published summaries, not the full papers. Check each number against its source before publishing.

## 4. Full script

Narration is plain text. Lines in *italics* are directions for visuals and on-screen cards.

### 0:00 Hook

*Black screen. A city skyline with every light off.*

Every step into this trap is a smart decision.

Nobody pushes you in or tricks you. You walk in on your own, and you feel clever the whole way.

Scientists have started drawing maps of this trap. So let's walk into it together and see where it closes.

Say, suppose, you let AI do everything for ten years.

*Title card.*

### 0:30 The world of 2041

*Sunrise. Contact lenses flicker on, and text floats in the air.*

It's 2041. You open your eyes and your lenses switch on.

A voice in your ear says good morning. This is Echo, your AI, and Echo has been busy.

While you slept, it answered forty emails in your voice. It argued with your landlord's AI and got your rent down. It read your blood from a tiny patch on your arm and chose your breakfast.

*Window view: drones in neat lanes, a building being printed layer by layer.*

Outside, delivery drones fly in tidy lines, like ants in the sky. Across the street, a machine is printing a new building. It wasn't there yesterday.

You don't drive or shop any more, and you never wait in a queue. Honestly, it's wonderful.

So you make one small decision: "Echo, just handle everything."

*A counter appears: YEAR 1.*

### 1:45 Year 1: the Crutch Trap

At work, you are suddenly three times faster. Reports that took a week now take an afternoon. Your boss is thrilled and you get a raise.

Then one Tuesday, in a meeting, your lenses glitch for just ten seconds.

Someone turns to you and asks, "Can you walk us through your report?"

*Close on your face. Silence.*

You can't, even though you signed it, because you never really wrote it.

Here's the simple version of what happened. Imagine you join a gym, and you send a robot to lift the weights for you. The weights go up every day and the numbers look great, but your arms never change.

*Card: "The Augmentation Trap", Caosun and Aral, MIT, 2026.*

Two researchers at MIT built a model of this. In their model, the practice you skip is the very thing that made you good. Skip enough of it, and you can end up worse off than if you never had the AI.

This one isn't only a model, either.

*Card: Strömberg, Lei and Wu, 2026. 26,811 students.*

One study followed almost twenty-seven thousand school students. When they started using AI, their homework scores went up 18 percent, and they finished 30 percent faster.

Then came the exams, where there is no AI. Within six months, their exam scores fell 20 percent.

Their homework had improved, and they had learned less.

*Counter: YEAR 3.*

### 3:00 Year 3: the Layoff Trap

Your company makes an announcement. Four hundred people are being replaced by AI agents.

It sounds cold, but the maths is simple. The work costs less and profits jump.

Every other company sees this and does the same thing. They would be foolish not to.

*Shopping street. Shops open, nobody inside.*

Then something strange happens. Sales start falling at your company and at every other one.

The reason is that the people who lost their jobs were also the people buying things.

Think of a concert. One person stands up to see better, and it works for them. So the next person stands, and the next. Soon everyone is standing, nobody can see any better than before, and everybody's legs hurt.

*Card: "The AI Layoff Trap", Falk and Tsoukalas, 2026.*

Two economists wrote this down as a model. Each company keeps all the savings from a layoff, while the lost customers are shared out across everyone. So every company keeps going, even though it hurts them all.

They tested the usual fixes, including retraining and paying everyone a basic income. In their model, none of those stopped the race, and only one thing did: a tax on automation itself.

Remember, that is one model, not a measurement. But look at its shape. Every company made the smart move, and together it was a terrible one.

*Counter: YEAR 6.*

### 4:15 Year 6: the Split

By now, you notice the city has quietly become two cities.

*Two escalators side by side, one rising, one sinking.*

Picture two escalators next to each other. One goes up and one goes down. They look the same, and you stepped onto one years ago without noticing.

Some people use Echo like a coach. They ask it to explain, they argue with it, and they get sharper every year.

Most people use Echo like a crutch. It gives the answer and they pass it along, and each year they can do a little less without it.

*Card: "The AI Skill Trap", Zhifeng Cai, 2026.*

An economist modelled exactly this. In his model there is a line. Start above it, and you keep climbing. Start below it, and you slide toward depending on AI for everything.

Both groups are using the very same machine.

*Counter: YEAR 10.*

### 5:15 Year 10: the Slow Handover

The city is voting on a huge new plan, which will decide where the water goes, where the power goes, and who gets what.

The plan is nine hundred pages long.

*A wall of text scrolling past faces lit by lenses.*

Nobody reads it. Why would they? Everyone just asks their AI, "How should I vote?"

Here's the part nobody mentions: the plan was written by AIs too.

So machines wrote it, machines read it, and machines advised on it. Then a human pressed the button.

Humans still press the button, but nobody is really choosing.

*A steering wheel. One screw falls out, then another.*

Imagine a steering wheel held on by a hundred screws. Take one out, and nothing changes. Take out another, and still nothing changes. You keep going, and at some point you turn the wheel and the car doesn't answer.

*Card: "Gradual Disempowerment", Kulveit and others, 2025.*

A group of researchers argue this is the real risk. In their picture there is no robot army and no evil machine, only a slow handover of one small decision at a time, until people aren't steering any more.

That paper is an argument, not an experiment. But ask yourself how many screws you have already taken out.

### 6:30 The blackout

*The sky ripples green.*

Then, on an ordinary afternoon, the sky turns green.

A storm from the Sun slams into Earth, and every AI on the planet goes dark.

*Lenses go blank. Drones settle on rooftops like tired birds.*

Your lenses turn into plain glass. Echo goes silent. The drones land wherever they can.

For the first time in ten years, the city is quiet.

*A control room. One red light flashing.*

You are at the water plant when the alarm starts. A pump is failing. If it stops, half the city has no water by night.

On the wall hangs a paper manual, with diagrams and arrows and tiny numbers. Twelve people stare at it.

*Card: "Your Brain on ChatGPT", MIT Media Lab, 2025. Small early study.*

One small study at MIT once saw a tiny version of this moment. People who wrote essays with AI struggled to quote what they had written minutes earlier. The words had never really passed through their heads.

Nobody in the room can follow the manual, except you.

Ten years ago you made yourself a quiet promise to do one hard thing a day by hand. Some days it was a page of sums, or a map without directions. Once it was a broken tap you fixed yourself.

It felt pointless at the time, but it helps you now.

*Your finger traces the diagram.*

You read the diagram slowly. You find the valve, a big rusty wheel that nobody has touched in years.

You turn it.

*The red light goes green.*

The alarm stops.

Forty-seven minutes later, the lights come back on.

### 8:00 The way out

*Lenses flicker on.*

"Hello," says Echo. "What did I miss?"

You could go straight back to how things were, and most people will.

*Card: "How AI Impacts Skill Formation", Shen and Tamkin, Anthropic, 2026.*

But here is the last piece of research, and it's the hopeful one.

Fifty-two programmers had to learn a brand new tool. Half used AI. On the test afterwards, the AI group scored about 50 percent, and the group without it scored about 65.

But look closer. Some people in the AI group did fine. They were the ones who asked the AI to explain why, and not only to hand over the answer.

So the trap has a door, and it was never locked.

*Back to you, looking out at the city.*

"New setting, Echo. Don't give me the answer first. Ask me what I think."

Echo pauses, then says, "Okay. What do you think?"

*Slow pull back over the lit city.*

### 9:00 Close

A crutch and a coach can be the same machine on different settings.

The research doesn't say AI makes you weaker. It says that letting it do your thinking does.

So here's my question for you. What is the one thing you still do the hard way?

Tell me in the comments.

*End card: "This was a suppose story. The studies on the cards are real, and links are in the description."*

## 5. Visual direction

Cinematic, not a conventional explainer. The early years must look like the best life the viewer has ever seen, because the trap only works if it is attractive.

| Act | Light and colour | Camera | The one image to land |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hook | Black, then a dark skyline | Static | A city with no lights |
| 2041 world | Warm sunrise, white and gold | Smooth glides | A building being printed |
| Year 1 | Same warmth, slightly too perfect | Slow push-ins | Your face when the lens glitches |
| Year 3 | Colour draining toward grey-blue | Wide and still | Open shops with nobody inside |
| Year 6 | Split screen, same room twice | Locked off, mirrored | Two people, one machine |
| Year 10 | Cold, flat, evenly lit | Very slow drift | Faces lit by lenses, nobody talking |
| Blackout | Green sky, deep shadow | Handheld for the first time | A hand on a rusty valve wheel |
| Way out | Dawn, warm again but softer | Slow pull back | You, looking at the city without lenses |

### Three things to avoid

- **No dystopia in Years 1 to 3.** Bright morning light, nothing ominous.
- **No rich-versus-poor imagery in Year 6.** The split is about skill, not money. Show identical flats and identical lenses, with one person arguing with Echo and one just nodding.
- **No glowing city in the blackout.** Every screen, drone and lens overlay is dead, and the aurora is the only light.

### Future tech to show

- Contact lenses that overlay the world
- An in-ear assistant that speaks in your voice
- AI agents negotiating with each other
- Drone delivery lanes
- Buildings printed overnight
- Breakfast chosen from a health patch

### Research cards

Same design every time: paper title, authors, year, and the type of evidence. They appear over silence or room tone, so the numbers read as real and separate from the fiction.

## 6. Sound direction

Echo's voice stays warm from start to finish. If it turns cold, the audience blames the machine, and the point is that the machine never changed.

- **2041 and Year 1:** soft pads, gentle chimes, Echo close in the ear.
- **Year 3:** remove the human sounds one at a time: footsteps, chatter, shop doors.
- **Year 6:** one short musical loop repeating, slightly too regular.
- **Year 10:** almost nothing except room tone and a faint hum.
- **Blackout:** cut everything mid-note. Hold three full seconds of silence before narration returns, then only real sounds: the alarm, breathing, metal creaking.
- **Way out:** wind, birds, distant traffic. No music until the final line.
