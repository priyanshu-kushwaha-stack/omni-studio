# v5 cut, frame by frame: three Cursor reviewers, no house rules (2026-11-15)

The owner asked for this: "Forget all the rules we have set so far and then evaluate the video completely on its merit…
how we can target our audience and keep them hooked for every frame."

**How it was run.** Three reviewers in Cursor worked separately: GPT-5.6 Sol (xhigh), Gemini 4 Argon (high) and Claude Opus 5.5 (high). None was given the channel's rules, taste notes or room history. Each had:

- the cut as 50 sheets of timestamped frames: one frame every 0.5 s for 0:00-0:30, then one every 2 s to the end;
- the timed transcript;
- a description of the sound.

Every reviewer opened all 50 sheets. Their full reviews, the brief and the transcript are in `room/g2/cursor-v5/`.

Below is the merge. "3/3" means all three reviewers raised the point independently. I checked the frames for every disputed picture claim; the result is noted where it matters.

## The verdict (3/3)

**No: this cut won't hold a what-if audience.** All three reviewers praise the premise, the brother, the crumbs, the escalation in the middle and the ending twist. All three also say these are buried in a 13:22 film that has about 10 minutes of story. They aim for 9:45, 9:50 and 10:15-10:30.

The film loses people in three places:

1. **0:00-0:31.** No voice for 7.4 s. The premise image flashes at 0:03 at about a tenth of the frame. Then comes the plasma law. The title question isn't asked until 0:31.
2. **1:18-2:38.** A late title card, then a leaflet that re-explains the deal the viewer has just watched. All three name this the biggest drop.
3. **The fact detours.** The sleep science (6:18-7:05), plasma and the border (8:21-8:41), the Mechanical Turk (8:57-9:10), and Folding@home in the middle of the twist (10:53-11:18).

One reviewer's rough guess is that about 18-20% of viewers reach the twist at 10:40. The reviewers call the twist the film's best idea.

## The story

**Keep (3/3):**

- the premise picture: the pale "you" typing over your sleeping body while coins float into the jar;
- the brother and his running lines: "What'd you get?", "Scam.", "That's a shoe.", "You live here.", "Explains the smell.", "I'll fail something. You can come to the retake.";
- the crumbs as a fair-play mystery, and their payoffs: "Is that a shoe?" and the same face drawn badly the same way;
- the world reacting from 4:37 to 5:35;
- "You can't sleep on it. Sleeping is the job.";
- "The biggest idea people ever had together, and nobody was awake for it.";
- "Nobody's asleep. And everybody's typing."

**What's broken:**

1. **The title says AI; the film never does (Opus, Gemini).** The renter is always "a company", and the machine at 11:27 is never named. Fix: "So what if an **AI** could rent your brain while you slept?", and name the client in the twist.
2. **The twist and the final sting contradict each other (Opus, Gemini).** At 11:34 the job is "design the thing that will never need sleepers". At 12:57 the broker sells a band for daytime, which still needs people. Both reviewers propose the same fix: **the drawing the sleepers built is the daytime band, designed for the AI.** Refusing then means something, the awake street becomes the proof, and the film finally says "AI".
3. **The stakes are told, not shown (3/3).** "What your family owes" is never a number. Opus points out that every kitchen shot already has a yellow note on the fridge. Make it "OWE $14,200" and move it: down at 3:18 and 7:51, back up after the crash at 8:20. GPT adds: seed the brother's graduation in the first minute, so losing it at 7:22 hurts.
4. **"You" is passive until 9:40 (GPT).** Things happen to you; the first real choice is the contract.
5. **Too many trips out of the story for facts (3/3).** There are nine: DishBrain, Loewi, mice, rats, plasma, Mexico, Mechanical Turk, the sleep statistic and Folding@home. Keep two or three, short:
   - DishBrain in 5 s, because it makes the premise believable;
   - sleep cleaning and filing the day in about 12-20 s, because that makes the graduation loss land;
   - plasma and the border in about 8 s.

   Folding@home inside the twist is the worst-placed thing in the film.
6. **Plant an open loop (Opus).** At 1:04, "And that's the problem" should become "The best wage of your life. And there are three problems with it. The third, nobody on your street sees coming." The Problem 1/2/3 labels are already in the film.
7. **Problems 1 and 2 overlap (Gemini).** Make them distinct:
   - Problem 1 is the rush (everyone sleeps for money);
   - Problem 2 is the cost (keys, PIN, door, the graduation);
   - Problem 3 is the crash.
8. **A plot hole at 9:40 (Gemini).** The warehouse has just said "rested ones only", and you haven't really slept in six weeks, yet the broker offers you triple pay. Add one line saying why it's you (for example, you can stay under twelve hours straight, so the build needs you).
9. **Plant the same face in both notebook columns at 4:03, without comment (Opus),** so the reveal at 10:32 feels earned.
