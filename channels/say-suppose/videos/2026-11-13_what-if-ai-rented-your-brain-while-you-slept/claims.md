# Claims ledger: What If AI Rented Your Brain While You Slept? (G1)

Started at G1 (2026-11-13) for every real-world fact the packaging may state, so the claims check can read the sources;
G2 adds a row for every real-world claim the script speaks. Sources were fetched and read on 2026-11-13 (a research
agent, curl and WebFetch); quotes are verbatim from the fetched text. Rows are numbered in research order; a row the
packaging does not use stays here for G2.

The story is invented and carries no row: the band, the rental companies, the pay per hour, the crumbs, the shared
mind and what it is paid to build, you, your family and friends, every night count and every dollar figure in the story.

**The brief's three "verify" facts, now sourced:** US paid plasma and the border centres (c01-c06, c22, c23;
ProPublica 2019 and 2022, the eCFR, NPR 2021); the 2021 Coors dream-incubation stunt and the scientists' open letter
(c07, c08; Harvard Petrie-Flom, UC Irvine); AI data-labelling pay (c09, narrowed to TIME's one documented case: Kenyan
workers labelling for OpenAI through Sama, $1.32-$2 an hour).

**Corrected from the brief:** "the US supplies most of the world's plasma" is "about two thirds" (c03; ProPublica 2022
says about 60%, so "more than half" is the safe floor; never 70%); Loewi's dream was in 1920, published 1921 (c21);
Gardner's "speech" problems have no source (c15 keeps "his thinking began to suffer"); the Pong cells showed "apparent
learning", they did not "learn to play" (c19); reCAPTCHA's 2008 paper says "more than 100 million" a day (c16);
Mechanical Turk closed on 30 September 2026, so it is spoken in the past tense (c17); Xie 2013 is in mice (c12).

## Dropped

- **Gardner, speech problems**: no fetched source mentions speech. Only cognitive and sensory effects (BBC) and concentration and short-term memory (Wikipedia) are sourced.
- **"200 million CAPTCHAs a day" credited to von Ahn's Science 2008 paper**: the paper says "more than 100 million". 200 million appears only in NYT 2011's report of the group's estimate (c16).
- **"70%" US share of world plasma**: no fetched source supports it. The sources say two thirds (all plasma, NPR and Annals of Blood), 74% (source plasma, 2016) or about 60% (ProPublica 2022).
- **"Learned to play Pong"** (as a flat claim): the paper says "apparent learning". Kept in narrowed form (c19).
- **"Glymphatic" credited to Xie 2013's quote**: the abstract doesn't use the word. The 60% finding is kept (c12).
- **The dream-advertising letter's own page** (dxe.pubpub.org/pub/dreamadvertising) blocked us with a 403. **Google's 2009 reCAPTCHA blog post** returned a bot check. Neither was read, so neither is quoted directly. Their facts are covered by Petrie-Flom, UCI and NYT.
- **"At rest"** in the brain-energy claim: not in Raichle's quote, so dropped from the wording (c10).
- Also seen but not needed: Gizmodo's 2021 piece on the dream letter (via WebFetch summary) says "The ad didn't run for nearly 100 million Super Bowl viewers." It disputes the letter's reach claim. Worth knowing if the script says the Coors stunt reached a Super Bowl audience. It didn't air during the game.
