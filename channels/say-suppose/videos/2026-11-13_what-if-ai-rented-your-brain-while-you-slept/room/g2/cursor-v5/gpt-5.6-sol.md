**No: this cut will not hold the target audience, despite an excellent premise and a strong final-act reveal.**

## 4. Story

### Does it work?

The underlying story works. It has a genuinely strong escalation:

1. Desperate person sells an apparently unused bodily resource.
2. Easy money produces amusing mental “crumbs.”
3. Sleep becomes labor.
4. The individual loses memory and identity.
5. The market industrializes and prices collapse.
6. Vulnerable people become both labor and product.
7. The separate brains were secretly one collective mind.
8. They were building the machine that would replace them.

That final reveal is excellent. It answers the early “Why not use a computer?” question, pays off the repeated “one brain, one band, one jar” promise, and turns the crumbs from jokes into evidence.

### What does not work

- **The protagonist is passive for nine minutes.** Events happen to “you”; the first substantial decision is the premium contract at 9:40.
- **The family debt is asserted, not dramatized.** The premium contract promises to clear it, but the audience has never seen the bill, eviction threat, medical cost, or why the brother’s graduation matters financially.
- **The brother becomes the emotional anchor too late.** Graduation at 7:22 is powerful material that should be seeded in the opening minute.
- **Three documentary detours compete with the fable:** DishBrain, Nobel-dream history, and Mechanical Turk. One or two concise facts would add credibility; all three slow the film.
- **The three “problems” arrive after the audience has already understood them.** The labels feel like essay headings rather than turns in a story.
- **The final personal refusal does not alter the world.** That is thematically valid, but the daytime-rental sting needs to be the actual ending—not setup for a CTA.

### What to keep

- “Computers cost money to run. You’re already lying there.”
- The shoe craving, shared tune, fitted sheet, mystery bridge word, and second-band experiment.
- The city asleep by seven and nobody awake to watch the news.
- Forgotten keys, PIN, and front door.
- The brother’s graduation and the lost memory of attending it.
- The falling price, warehouse, and “rested ones only” discrimination.
- The premium contract and midnight deadline.
- The crumbs assembling into a blueprint.
- “The job … is to design the thing that will never need sleepers.”
- “The biggest idea people ever had together, and nobody was awake for it.”
- “Nothing. The best deal on the street.”
- The final revelation that nobody is asleep and everybody is typing.

### Better structure: about 10:20

1. **0:00–0:20 — Cold open:** Band activates, pale brain works, `$90` appears, protagonist cannot remember the word “keys.” “Tonight, AI rented your sleeping brain.”
2. **0:20–0:55 — Need:** Plasma chair, tiny payment, overdue debt and brother’s graduation expense. Broker offers a better sale.
3. **0:55–1:30 — Rules:** One concise visual explanation: sleep, work, hourly payment, no memories, supposedly isolated brains.
4. **1:30–2:30 — Fun and seduction:** Shoe, tune, fitted sheet, mystery word, brother’s second band. Money accumulates.
5. **2:30–3:35 — Society follows:** Nurse sleeps, boss opens nap room, grandmother wins, warehouse fills, city goes to bed.
6. **3:35–4:25 — Cost:** Twelve-hour nights; keys, PIN, and home disappear. Explain sleep’s cleaning/filing function in one vivid metaphor.
7. **4:25–5:20 — Personal wound:** Seeded graduation arrives. The protagonist attends, then sells the filing night and cannot remember it.
8. **5:20–6:20 — Market crash:** Prices fall; border traffic and industrial sleep expand; brother is rejected as insufficiently rested.
9. **6:20–7:20 — Irresistible offer:** Premium contract clears the visible family debt. Midnight deadline. Brother stops the signature with the crumbs.
10. **7:20–8:40 — Reveal:** Shared clues prove all sleepers are one mind. Their work is a blueprint for a machine that will replace them.
11. **8:40–9:35 — Choice:** Protagonist rejects the contract, puts away the band, and receives one uninterrupted night.
12. **9:35–10:10 — Coda:** Individual dream, labels removed, clinic refusal, then the broker’s daytime offer and a street of awake workers.
13. **10:10–10:20 — End:** “Nobody’s asleep. Everybody’s typing.” Cut to black. Put the CTA on a silent end card.
