## What to write (markdown, return it as your whole answer)
1. **Verdict in five lines:** would this hold the audience? The predicted retention shape (where the big drops are, with times).
2. **The first 60 seconds, second by second:** what the viewer sees and hears, whether the title's promise is met, and what to change. Be specific to the timestamps.
3. **Minute-by-minute retention map:** for every minute of the film, a hook score 1-10, the moment most likely to lose people (timestamp), and why.
4. **Story:** does the story itself work? Premise payoff, escalation, stakes, character, clarity, the ending. If the story should be restructured, propose the better structure concretely (beat list with rough times), including any scenes to cut, merge, move or add, and a better ending if needed. Say what to keep because it works.
5. **Picture, frame by frame:** concrete problems you saw in frames (cite the timestamp): confusing or static shots, held frames, repeated shots, unreadable text, empty or unclear compositions, cards that stay too long, places the picture does not show what the words say, and where a visual gag or a stronger image would carry more.
6. **Dialogue and narration:** lines that are slow, unclear, explain too much, or could be funnier or sharper, with the rewrite.
7. **Pacing and length:** is 13:22 right? What would you cut to reach the length you recommend, by timestamp?
8. **Action items:** a numbered list, ordered by impact on retention, each one with the timestamp(s), the change, and why it keeps people watching. Mark each as STORY, PICTURE, LINE, SOUND or PACE. Aim for 25-40 items.

Be blunt and concrete. "Make it more engaging" is useless; "0:41-0:52 holds one static shot of the band for 11 s while the narrator explains it; cut to 4 s and show the money landing" is useful.
