# Frame-by-frame review: "What If AI Rented Your Brain While You Slept?" (cut at 13:22)

## 1. Verdict in five lines

1. The premise, the brother and the world-building are strong. In its current form, though, this cut will **not** hold a what-if audience, because it buries a 9-minute film inside 13:22 of leaflets, fact cards and held frames.
2. The title promises **AI**. The narration never says the word: the renter is always "a company", and the machine at 11:27 is never named.
3. Predicted retention shape (my estimate, not data): about 65% at 0:30, a cliff from 1:18 to 2:35 (title card, then a 70-second leaflet lecture) down to about 35%, small dips at 4:15, 5:36 and 6:18–7:05, another slide from 8:21 to 9:10 (plasma, border and Mechanical Turk facts), and about 18–20% reaching the twist at 10:40.
4. The twist and the ending are good enough to deserve far more viewers than will reach them. But the payoff image at 11:22–11:32 can't be read, and the two best ending images (12:10–12:21) are spoken but never shown.
5. Fix: put the band image and the "AI" promise in the first 10 seconds, plant an open loop about "three problems", cut nine fact digressions down to two or three, and bring the film to about 9:50.

## 4. Story

**What works (keep it):**
- **The premise mechanics.** A pale "you" typing over your sleeping body while coins float into a jar is an instantly readable metaphor and a great recurring image.
- **The brother.** "What'd you get?" / "Scam." is a real running gag. It pays off at 12:04 ("Scam. Told you.") and 12:08 ("Come back Thursday"). "I'll fail something. You can come to the retake" (7:59) is the best line in the film.
- **The crumbs** as a fair-play mystery (shoe, tune, fitted sheet, a word that isn't yours), and their payoff at 10:14 ("Is that a shoe?") and 10:32 (the same face, drawn badly the same way).
- **Societal escalation from 4:30 to 5:35.** Pure what-if joy: the boss's nap room with a till, the grandmother out-earning the street, the city in bed by seven, "Nobody's up to watch it."
- **The decision scene from 9:40 to 10:01.** Concrete stakes (clear the debt, no more Thursdays) and a killer line: "You can't sleep on it. Sleeping is the job."
- **The callbacks in the ending.** The bridge dream "in your own colours", and the KEYS / DOOR / YOU labels.

**What doesn't:**

1. **The title promise.** "What if AI rented your brain" becomes "what if a company rented your brain". The AI is only implied by "the thing that will never need sleepers". The viewer who clicked for AI waits 11 minutes and still never hears it.

2. **The twist and the sting contradict each other.** At 11:34 the job is "to design the thing that will never need sleepers", which most viewers will hear as "an AI that replaces you". At 12:57 the broker says "This one's for daytime" and the street is awake and typing. So the machine is a *daytime band*, which still needs people. Pick one. My recommendation: **the drawing is a band that works while you're awake, designed for the AI client**. Then the refusal means something (you'd be building the thing that takes your days as well as your nights), the epilogue becomes the proof, and "AI" is in the film.

3. **The stakes are told, not shown.** "What it owes" (0:27) is never a number. There is already a yellow sticky note on the fridge in every kitchen shot. Make it "OWE $14,200" and let it go down (3:18, 7:51) and back up after the price crash (8:20). That gives the viewer a running scoreboard for free.

4. **There is no early open loop.** "And that's the problem" at 1:04 is the place to promise three problems, with the third being one nobody sees coming. The film already uses Problem 1/2/3 labels, so promise the countdown up front.

5. **The film keeps stepping out of the story for facts.** There are nine of them: DishBrain 2:11, Loewi 4:15, mice 6:35, rats 6:49, plasma 8:21, Mexico 8:35, Mechanical Turk 8:57, sleep statistic 9:10, Folding@home 10:53. Keep three:
   - DishBrain at 5 s, because it makes the premise plausible.
   - Brain clean-up and filing at about 12 s, because it powers the graduation payoff.
   - Plasma and the border at 8 s, because it grounds the hero.

   Folding@home *in the middle of the twist* is the worst placement in the film.

6. **Clarity slips.** "Midnight. The band goes on" (0:45) comes after the band is already on. "Three notes from the chair" (3:16) is unclear. "He gets out the stamp" (7:07) is shown as stickers. "He opens the laptop" (10:36) never says whose crumbs are on the screen.
