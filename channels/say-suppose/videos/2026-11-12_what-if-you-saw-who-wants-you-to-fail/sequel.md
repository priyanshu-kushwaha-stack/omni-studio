# Sequel: What If Everyone Saw How You Really Felt?

## The hook (the last scene, no new rule)

The kitchen, the morning after results. Two letters on the fridge, six years apart, and the brushes still in the jar. Min-jun at the door with a letter of his own, grinning, and he says it to your face. A paper snap above your own head, out of frame. His grin stops. His eyes go up and read something, and we never see the number. The film ends on his face changing. The viewer who comes back for Part 2 remembers one thing: the meter opened over you, and your best friend read it before you could.

## The rule twist, one line

The same meter, turned round: it opens over your head whenever someone tells you their good news to your face, and everyone in the room can read it but you.

## Five beats for Part 2

1. **The door.** Min-jun's letter, his news said out loud, and the snap above you. Ji-yoon reads it from the fridge; her face does the thing his did. Your habit, planted in Part 1, is that you say congratulations first and fastest. The change hits exactly that: now your face says it before your mouth does, and you have no idea what it says. Title question by 0:10: what if everyone could see how you really felt?
2. **Everyone brings you news.** The montage turns at every use. Tae-ho tells you his score just to watch the card; Ms. Kang says "Good" and glances up; the convenience-store clerk tells you about his daughter and smiles at something above your head. People start saying things to your face to read you, and the group chat fills with numbers you never saw. The gap every time: you expect your own face to be plus one hundred, and the room's eyes say it isn't.
3. **The limit, mirrored.** Only out loud, only to a face: so you start taking news by text, and asking people to call. The meters go quiet because you've stopped letting anyone look. Min-jun notices first: he stops telling you things. The loophole from Part 1, the screen you could keep her on, is now the screen you keep yourself behind. Open loop: the person who can't see your number is the only one who still wants to.
4. **Her news.** The cost, at 60%. Ji-yoon, with the scholarship money freeing her nights, takes the brushes out of the jar and says it to your face: she's applied, and she's in. The snap above your head. She reads it, and her arms stay up. You can't see it, and you can't ask, because asking is admitting there's something to read. Dilemma at 75%: ask her what she saw, and live with the answer; or never ask, and keep the hug you think you still have.
5. **Say it again.** The pay-off is the gag turned round. On the dock at six, you ask her to say it again, to your face, and you let her read you twice. The second card opens on whatever the first one said, and this time she says the number out loud, and what she thinks it's about, and she's wrong about the why too. A tag has one colour for every kind of hurt, and so does a face. The residue, inside the last image of the empty jar: tomorrow someone will tell you their good news, and for half a second your face will answer before you do, and they'll see it, and stay anyway.
