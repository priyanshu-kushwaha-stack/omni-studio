## Why Seoul, a senior's autumn

The meter needs a place where a friend's win is your loss by arithmetic. South Korea's college entrance exam (the CSAT,
"Suneung") grades most subjects on a nine-grade relative curve, and grade 1 is the top 4% of the cohort; English and
Korean history are graded on fixed cut-offs instead (Wikipedia, "College Scholastic Ability Test"; a prep guide for the
4% line, c01). The school record ("naesin") used the same top-4% band until the 2025 high-school entrants moved to a
five-tier scale whose top tier is the top 10% (Seoul Economic Daily, June 2026; a 2028-cycle grading guide; c13). A
senior in autumn 2026 started high school in 2024, so the film's year is the last one graded on the four-per-cent school
curve: in a class, a handful of seats.

The neighbourhood: Daechi-dong in Gangnam, famous for its cram schools, "over 950" in Wikipedia's (2011) count and
"close to a thousand" in ABC's (2014) (c04). Seoul's ordinance lets hagwon and private tutors teach only between 5 a.m.
and 10 p.m. (Korea JoongAng Daily, December 2025, on a debated proposal to extend it to midnight; outcome not
confirmed), and TIME (2011) followed district officials on a late-night patrol (c03). So at ten every academy on the
street lets out at once, which is the film's thousand-meter picture.

Exam day: in November 2025 Korea rescheduled about 140 flights, including 75 international (TRT World; other outlets
say 65, so the split is not spoken), during a 1:05-1:40 p.m. window for the English listening test, and the stock market
traded an hour later; students running late can be taken to their halls by police motorbike (Wikipedia) (c02). The 2026
exam (19 November) plans a 25-minute window, so the film says "last year".

Rejected: Kota, India (the same lanes and coaching-sign streets, but no rule that makes a friend's grade your loss, and
no day the country stops flying); the brief's Pangyo startup, awards broadcast and Paris fellowship (a time skip, and
Wasserman's co-founder figure was not checked).

## The envy research the film leans on

- **Comparison runs to people near you.** Festinger (1954), Corollary III A: given a range of people to compare with,
  "someone close to one's own ability or opinion will be chosen" (c10). Envy arises toward "a superior other, in a
  domain that is important to their self-view, and if the other was initially similar to them" (van de Ven, Zeelenberg
  and Pieters 2012, citing Salovey and Rodin 1984), and people "do not like to admit being maliciously envious, neither
  to themselves nor to others" (same paper, citing Foster 1972) (c12). The rival at minus six and the thousand zeros.
- **Closeness sharpens it.** Tesser, Millar and Moore (1988): when someone outperforms you on a task relevant to you,
  "the closer the other the greater the threat"; on an irrelevant task, the closer the other, the more you bask (c06).
- **The pain region.** Takahashi et al. (2009, Science): when another person's possession was "superior and
  self-relevant", envy and anterior cingulate activity were stronger; that activity predicted ventral striatum
  activity when the envied person met misfortune (Europe PMC abstract). The ACC "also shows activation during pain, such
  as empathetic pain or the pain associated [with] social exclusion", and the ventral striatum is "typically associated
  with rewarding stimuli" (ScienceBlogs summary) (c05). The film says "one brain-scan study" and "a region also active
  in pain"; never "the pain centre" or "dorsal".
- **Two kinds of envy.** Van de Ven, Zeelenberg and Pieters (2009, Emotion): benign envy leads to "a moving-up
  motivation", malicious envy to "a pulling-down motivation" (c07). Min-jun's notebook.
- **Good news and the people you love.** Gable, Reis, Impett and Asher (2004): partners who meet good news
  enthusiastically go with higher intimacy and daily satisfaction (c09).
- **The life you almost had.** Medvec, Madey and Gilovich (1995): at Barcelona 1992 bronze medallists looked happier
  than silver; silver's nearest alternative is gold, bronze's is no medal (c08). Roese and Summerville (2005): across
  eleven studies education is the first life regret, 32.2% of all regrets, career second at 22.3% (c11). Ji-yoon.

## Dropped

The brief's "Parrott and Smith: over 75% conceal envy behind praise or protective advice": no such figure or finding in
Parrott and Smith (1993), whose abstract finds envy marked by "inferiority, longing, resentment, and disapproval of the
emotion". The brief's 1,500 hagwons and "densest" (c04 is about a thousand), and "dorsal ACC".
