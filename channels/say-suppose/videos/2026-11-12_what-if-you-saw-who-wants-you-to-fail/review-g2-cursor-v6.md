# Outside review of cut v6 (Cursor, GPT-5.6 Sol Extra High), 2026-11-14

One reviewer, alone, on 50 contact sheets (one frame per second of the 13:11 cut), the timed script, the script, the story rules, packaging, taste.md and the round 16-17 review. Brief: `BRIEF.md` of the review pack. Not applied yet: the owner decides.

# Overall verdict

V6 fixes the opening structurally: `-84%` lands by `0:03`, the title question begins at `0:04`, and the stakes finish by roughly `0:17`. The jump to the platform is understandable because “It started this morning” names the rewind.

The main problem is execution speed. This 13:11 cut contains many 5–10 second stretches where one composition carries several narration clauses. The story escalates correctly—one face, class, hall, street, country, window—but the picture repeatedly falls back into diagrams, static lineups and held reaction shots. The window is a strong conceptual peak, but its reflection logic and camera movement need to be much clearer. The cliffhanger is good; the long CTA hold weakens it.

## Does each set piece top the last?

Structurally, yes:

1. Stranger and one card.
2. Rival, teacher, mirror and eleven strangers.
3. Class-wide mock-score weapon.
4. Five-hundred-seat hall.
5. Thousand-person street.
6. Country-scale exam silence.

The scale ladder is correct. The energy between those set pieces is not.

## Where energy drops

- **`1:04–1:32`:** Rule explanation repeats the hug and relies on held cards.
- **`4:02–5:24`:** Peer-comparison diagrams, Problem 1 and the sister’s back create a long explanatory stretch.
- **`5:25–6:23`:** Curve grid, paper stacks, phone lists and repeated class framings turn the “weapon” into slides.
- **`6:47–7:11`:** News loophole and inspector fact stall immediately before the thousand-card sequence.
- **`8:17–9:12`:** Study-room walk, notebook pages and the brain-scan slide form the largest mid-film retention valley.
- **`9:20–9:50`:** The board diagram and auditorium-like “backs” staging delay what should be a fast ambush.
- **`10:38–10:52`:** The runway fact holds too long and feels documentary.
- **`11:04–11:19`:** National desk grid and map repeat the infographic problem.
- **`12:00–12:16`:** The peak becomes visually repetitive rather than continuously revealing.

## Is the window a triumph?

Conceptually, yes. It solves the face limit, the mirror plant and the backs problem at once. Execution currently weakens it because:

- Reflections are not unmistakably reflections.
- Physical backs and reflected faces are not clearly separated.
- The camera appears to jump between window panes.
- The card wall holds too long without new information.
- The teal transformation is not visually dominant enough.

A continuous push into genuinely reversed cards, followed by a teal wave and two crisp exceptions, would make it the film’s unambiguous triumph.

## Does the ending land as a cliffhanger?

The story beat does. The picture only partly does. The unseen snap, your eyeline and the dark window need stronger staging. The seven-second kitchen CTA then releases the tension instead of cutting on it.
