# Sequel: What If You Owed Twice as Much Every Night?

## The hook (the last scene, no new rule)

Day fifty-six, Lotte's counter. Jonas pays for both rolls, as he always has. He glances at his own phone as he puts the coins down, and his face changes. We never see the screen. He puts the phone away and says nothing. The film ends on your roll and Lotte's coin. The viewer who comes back for Part 2 remembers one thing: the man who always paid looked at his balance and went quiet.

## The rule twist, one line

The same rule, on the other side of zero: Jonas's account was one cent below nothing at midnight on day one, and a debt doubles exactly the way money did.

## Five beats for Part 2

1. **Minus two cents.** Jonas at the counter, day two of his own clock, phone up: minus 0,02, and the digits sink into the screen instead of peeling off it. He pays for your roll anyway. Lotte: "Counted?" He has never counted in his life; that is the habit the change hits.
2. **Nothing, nothing, a letter.** The montage turns at every use: an overdraft letter he frames as a joke, a bank app that stops opening, the day the tram turns his card down and you pay. The jaw drop: a debt that outgrows what he earns in a year, then in a lifetime, while the roll still costs three ninety.
3. **The bank's other question.** Herr Brandt asks Jonas in: "Who do you owe?" Nobody, the rule says. Except somebody is being paid every night, a little, by everyone. Brandt's own ledger has started to lean. The plant from Part 1 returns with its sign flipped: a correction can erase a credit, but a debt is a claim someone else holds, and that someone wants to know who.
4. **Lotte's counter, again.** The cost: Jonas cannot be at the counter at all, because anything he touches becomes owed. You, who know the number, do his counting for him, and find there is no exact zero on this side: a one-cent refund saves a creditor, but a one-cent debt at midnight is two. The person to lose is the one who paid for your bread for three years.
5. **The pay-off.** The ticking clock is the same three minutes before midnight, mirrored: not getting to zero but being caught there by someone who gives, not takes. You pay Jonas's roll money back, one cent at midnight, the one coin Lotte kept out of the till. Zero, doubled, is zero. The residue: a debt isn't a number. It's somebody agreeing to wait for you until tomorrow.
