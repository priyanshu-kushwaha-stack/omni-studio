> SITUATION: Berlin, a flat above Anna's bakery. One cent in your account, and Jonas pays for your roll every morning.
> DESIRE: Everything you ever wanted. Then: to stop it before it eats the city.
> CONFLICT: The money arrives faster than anything can be made to spend it on, and every doubling thins what everyone else holds, starting with Anna's bread.
> CHANGE: The note on your phone goes from "stop at a thousand" to "stop at zero".
> RESULT: Zero at midnight, with two seconds to spare. Then Jonas's phone rips.
> UNDER: If all the money were mine, who would still bake the bread?
> DARLING: "You always know the number."
> DARLING: "It isn't taken from anyone. Except it is. From everyone. A little. Every night."
> DARLING: "Zero, doubled, is zero."

## [00:00] Midnight

> MOOD: night
> SCENE: Midnight. Your flat above the bakery, the city lights through the big window, wide. Jonas asleep on the sofa under his coat. Your phone face up on the table: 0,01. Name tags as each first appears: YOU · JONAS, your flatmate, pays for your roll every morning.

> HOLD: 2 s. The paper rip, loud. On the lock screen a paper one-cent coin tears down the middle into two whole coins. A split-flap flaps once: DAY 2 above, 0,02 below.

Jonas: Did your phone just... rip?

Jonas: Two cents. Don't spend it all at once.

> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: Your face in the phone's glow. 0,02.

Two cents. Last night, one. You know that.
You always know the number.

What if your money doubled every day?
Every night. Whatever's there at midnight, twice by morning. From one cent. New money. Taken from no one. And it will not stop.

Sounds like the best thing that could ever happen to you. Let's see what it costs.

> SCENE: Title card: What If You Doubled Your Money Every Day? A synth hit, then the flap.

## [00:30] The Rule

> MOOD: morning
> SCENE: Morning, Anna's bakery from across the street, wide: the shop, the chalkboard, the flat above. Inside: you, phone up; Anna, the baker, behind the counter (name tag: ANNA, the baker, slips you a free roll every day).

There is no off switch. Except one. Exactly zero at midnight. Not a cent more. Not a cent less.

> CARD: THE RULE · Every midnight: × 2 · The only way out: exactly 0,00 at midnight

> SCENE: You stick a yellow note on the back of your phone and write on it: STOP AT 1.000 €.

So you make yourself a rule too. A sticky note. Stop at a thousand.

> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: After closing. Anna sets one grain of wheat on her grandfather's chessboard, two on the next, four. Then the grains spill off the board, off the counter, and the camera pulls back until the bakery is a speck under a dune of wheat.

A king, a chessboard. One grain on the first square, and double on every square after (c05). By the last square, the board owes more wheat than the whole world grows in over a thousand years (c11).

> CARD: IT'S TRUE · 64 squares of doubling: more wheat than the world grows in 1,000 years

For ten days, nothing happens. Cents. Then it does.

Day twenty-two. Every tram in the city. Free. All day. On you.

Day twenty-eight. You buy the building. Anna never pays rent again.

Day thirty-one. Ten million euros. A billion cents.

Jonas: Is that... all yours?

> SCENE: You cross out the sticky note again. STOP AT 1 BILLION.

## [03:20] What It Costs

> MOOD: day
> SCENE: A cash machine in the street: you type 10.737.418,24. DAILY LIMIT 500,00. Five notes come out.

So you spend. Faster than it grows. Can you?
First, the obvious trick. Take it all out in cash. The machine gives you five hundred euros. Move it to another bank? Still your money. It doubles there too.

> SCENE: Your phone, a shop app: BUY on an electric car, a tiny jet, a racehorse. Then midnight: what was left flips from six million to thirteen.

A car. A tiny jet. A racehorse. Four million, gone by dinner.
At midnight, what's left doubles anyway. Thirteen million. You shopped all day, and woke up richer.

> SCENE: A laptop, a share chart: you tap BUY and the line shoots up.

You're thinking it. Why not just invest it?
You try. You buy shares. The price shoots up. Because you're the one buying. Now you're richer twice.

> SCENE: Day thirty-five, dawn. A thousand delivery drones on the roofs of the street, BREAD-NET on a screen. Jonas with his phone. Anna in her doorway, arms folded.

Day thirty-five. A hundred and seventy million. Jonas has a new idea: Anna's rolls, delivered to the whole city. By drone. A thousand drones. You pay.

Anna: I have two hands and one oven. Eighty rolls a morning. I bake what I bake.

> HOLD: 3 s. A thousand drones lift off at once and darken the sky. Eighty carry a roll. Jonas's phone counts orders: 1,000, 10,000, 40,000.

You bought a thousand deliveries. You didn't make one more roll.

> CARD: PROBLEM 1 · You can't spend it

Money is a ticket for things people make. Your pile is a bigger ticket for the same bread.
And everything you spend has to land somewhere. It lands where you stand.

> MOOD: dusk
> SCENE: Day forty. The bakery's back door. Karl, the flour man, a sack half off his van, an invoice in his hand (name tag: KARL, the flour man, brings Anna her flour). Anna in the doorway.

Karl: Same flour. New price. Triple.

Karl: The whole street has money now. Everyone charges more. Everyone does.

> SCENE: The board: 1,40 wiped to 2,10, to 3,00, to 4,20.

Day forty. Prices rise first where the new money lands. Your street. One forty. Two ten. Three euros. Four twenty.

> CARD: WHERE THE MONEY LANDS · New money raises prices there first

> MOOD: lamp
> HOLD: 3 s. The film's one quiet beat. After closing. Anna at the counter with a small savings book, pencil moving down a column, slowing, stopping.

She counts what her savings will buy. Last month, a year of flour. Tonight, four months.

It isn't taken from anyone. Except it is. From everyone. A little. Every night.

## [05:30] Bigger

> MOOD: day
> SCENE: Day fifty. The whole city from above. Every phone in every window lights up at once: +1.000.000,00.

So you try the obvious thing. You give it away. Day fifty. Five trillion. A million euros to every phone in Berlin.

> HOLD: 3 s. Phones buzzing in every window. The tram stops in the street, its driver gone. Anna's board: 4,20, wiped, 42,00, wiped, 63,00.

Nobody goes to work. Why would you? And by Saturday, a roll costs sixty-three euros. Because every million was new money. Chasing the same rolls.

> CARD: PROBLEM 2 · You can't give it away

> SCENE: Jonas at the counter, coins in his hand, staring at the board: 63,00. You hold your phone out to him.

Jonas: Sixty-three? Since when?

> BUBBLE: You: "Take it. All of it."

Jonas: Not me.

> CARD: PROBLEM 3 · Whoever holds it makes the bread cost more

> SCENE: Two pictures, one after the other: a king on a camel throwing gold in Cairo; a Berlin baker's window in 1923 with a price tag of 200.000.000.000 marks; then Anna's board.

This has happened before. For real. In 1324, a king from Mali, Mansa Musa, gave away so much gold in Cairo that gold stayed cheap there for at least twelve years (c06).
Right here in Berlin, by late 1923, a loaf of bread cost about two hundred billion marks (c01). Anna's board is heading the same way. Give it all away? You hit zero. Berlin doesn't.

> CARD: IT'S TRUE · Berlin, late 1923: a loaf of bread, about 200 billion marks

> SCENE: Day fifty-two. A TV studio, the anchor at her desk, silent under the narration: BREAKING, a map of Berlin glowing gold.

Day fifty-two. You're on the news. Day fifty-three, every bank in Europe freezes your account. It doesn't matter. Frozen money doubles too.

> MOOD: night
> SCENE: Day fifty-five. The camera pulls out from the bakery to the city, to the whole Earth, a gold ring of digits wrapping it.

> TAG: DAY 55 · 180.143.985.094.819,84

Day fifty-five. The number passes what the whole world makes in a year. Every loaf, every car, every hour of work on Earth. About 128 trillion dollars (c09).

Never hit zero, and by day two hundred and sixty-seven your account holds more cents than the estimated number of hydrogen atoms in the observable universe (c12).

> CARD: IT'S TRUE · Day 267: more cents than the estimated hydrogen atoms in the observable universe (about 10^80)

> SCENE: Your phone in your hand. You peel off the sticky note and write a new number: STOP AT 0,00.

You look from the galaxies to Anna's dark bakery. The sticky note gets its last number.

## [07:15] Exactly Zero

> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: Eleven at night. The penthouse, the city below, the phone on speaker.

You call Herr Brandt. Now. The universe can wait. Berlin can't.

Herr Brandt: So it was a mistake.

> BUBBLE: You: "Yes."

> SCENE: At his desk across the city, Herr Brandt takes the stamped form out of his drawer, NOT A MISTAKE, and tears it in half.

Herr Brandt: Everything goes. Your wages as well. You understand?

Herr Brandt: Fifty minutes.

> TAG: 23:50 · 0,00

> HOLD: 2 s. The split-flap runs down, digit by digit, to 0,00.

Zero. Everything. Ten minutes to midnight. You breathe out.

> TAG: 23:57 · 0,01

Then the phone buzzes. A refund. From the tram app. From the day you paid for every tram in Berlin. One cent.

One cent at midnight is two. Two is four. And it all starts again. Three minutes.

> SCENE: You out of the tower and down the night street at a run. A vending machine: 1,00 €. A tram stop: FREE TODAY. Jonas's window: dark.

Who takes one cent, at three minutes to midnight? The machine wants a euro. The tram is still free. Jonas is asleep.

> SCENE: Anna at her glass door, keys in hand, locking up. She sees you running and opens it a hand's width.

Anna: All counted?

You count. One cent. Into her palm. Twenty-three fifty-nine and fifty-eight.

> TAG: 23:59 · 0,00

> HOLD: 4 s. The lock screen: the coin with nothing to tear. Midnight. The flap does not move. Music out, the one planned silence.

Zero, doubled, is zero.

> SCENE: A big hit of music. Anna looks at the cent in her palm.

Anna: Counted.
