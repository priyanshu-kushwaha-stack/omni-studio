# Reference: "What If Your GTA Money Was Real?" (watched 2026-11-15)

The owner, on the v5 cut: "The whole story feels very zoomed in in here." and this reference, "a similar story and it
has different storylines and different pattern also of narration. Could you take some samples from it". Watched in
Chrome: the full transcript (53 lines, 9:27, 18 chapters) and frames across the whole film. 3.4k views when watched,
so it is a craft reference, not proof that the pattern performs.

## What it does

1. **It covers a life, not a week.** Two a.m. on night one, then "Three years later", "Nine years later", "Fifteen
   years". Each chapter jumps forward and lands on a new place: the gaming room, Mom's house, a penthouse over the city,
   the beach, a rented stadium, a GTA heist, the office of monitors, Jay's door, a mansion party, a park on Sunday.
   Ours: 56 days, and 40 of 116 shots are at Lotte's counter.
2. **The camera is wide.** Small figures in big places: a skyline behind the window, the beach at sunset, the whole
   mansion with the party outside it. Close-ups only for a phone screen or a face at the turn. Ours: mostly medium
   shots at one counter, many cropped close on a speaker.
3. **The narration is a list of pictures.** One short line per purchase or event, each a new image and a punch: "A
   penthouse above the city. Your old room would fit in the closet." / "You rented a stadium. For six guys." /
   "Twenty-one million real dollars... on things that don't exist." Characters get one or two lines, never a long
   skit. Ours: eight-line dialogue scenes (the party, Karl at the back door).
4. **It promises the cost up front.** At 0:25: "Sounds like the best thing that could ever happen to you. Let's see what
   it costs." Then a second rule at 0:49 ("it worked both ways"), with a gag ("Note to self. No more jackets.").
5. **Wish-fulfilment first, a full minute of it.** A house for Mom, a first car, a penthouse, a friend's student loan,
   the ocean, a whole restaurant, a stadium, first class. The things everyone daydreams about, before any problem.
6. **A running prop that escalates.** A note on the monitor: "One billion. Then I stop." becomes ten, then a hundred
   billion. "The finish line moved. It always does."
7. **Real people as proof.** Howard Hughes, J. Paul Getty, Steve Jobs: three short true stories near the end.
8. **A closing line people quote.** The two writers at a billionaire's party: "I have something he'll never have.
   Enough."
9. **Few words on screen.** No fact cards or day tags; the phone's bank app (a red -$2,500,000.00) carries the numbers.

What not to take: its peak is a mother's death and its ending is a lesson about greed, the emotional film the owner
moved the channel away from on 2026-11-13 ("I want exciting stuff"). Take its scope, framing and narration pattern,
not its sadness.

## For our film (v6)

- Widen in time and place: weeks and places, not one counter (the bakery becomes home base, one scene in four).
- The first two minutes: pure wish-fulfilment montage, one line and one wide picture each.
- A running prop: a sticky note on your phone, "Stop at a million", crossed out and rewritten.
- Narration in single-line beats; dialogue cut to one or two lines per character per scene.
- "Let's see what it costs" by 0:30.
- Wide shots by default; cards and tags only where a number must land.
- Keep from v5: the doubling numbers, the loopholes, the drones, the planet, day 267, the midnight race, "Counted.",
  Jonas's phone.
