## Premise

A faint ghost of the world one minute from now lies over everything you look at: the ghost bus arrives, the ghost chai spills. It shows what happens if you do nothing; the moment you act on it, the ghost redraws; you cannot switch it off, so you never again see anything for the first time, and you are never quite looking at now.
The ghost is invented. The real-world part, to be sourced at G2: what you see is already about a tenth of a second old by the time your visual system has processed it, and some scientists think your brain guesses forward to cover the gap; what a few seconds of warning let a train do by itself; how one rounded number gave Lorenz a different weather; and why a predicted reward lands as nothing.

## The viewer

Riya (`audience.md`), 22, Pune, final year, a result due this week, an internship whose boss messages late; a mother who calls twice a day, a grandfather who tells the same story at every visit, a younger cousin. Here "you" are the person who checks everything before it happens: the bus app, the result page, the typing dots. The film gives that habit its wish. Every gag comes from Riya's week: the chai stall, the canteen TV, the boss's late messages, the metro, the office card game, Sunday at grandfather's. The English packaging stays readable worldwide: no `₹`, no local slang.

## Five lines

> SITUATION: Final year, the result due Friday, and you check everything before it happens: the bus app, the result page, the typing dots.
> DESIRE: To never be caught out again; to know what is coming before it comes.
> CONFLICT: A ghost of the next minute lies over everything you look at, it redraws every time you act on it, and you cannot switch it off.
> CHANGE: You learn the ghost never showed the future, only a world where you stood still, and that the one thing it took from you was the first time of everything.
> RESULT: You close your eyes so your grandfather's punchline can land, and the phone stays in your pocket at the bus stop.
> UNDER: Would I rather know what's coming, or have it happen to me?

## How it differs from our earlier time films

The pause film stopped the world and drained it grey; the last-time film hung a counter over one head. Here nothing stops and nothing counts down: the world doubles, a pale second copy one minute ahead, and the device adds a layer instead of taking colour away. The person at risk is the grandfather, not the mother, and what you take from him is a laugh, not his life.

The brief's third jump was a crossing with a scooter and a child, which is the pause film's rescue. I replaced it with the metro platform at rush hour: no vehicle threatens anyone; the danger is a crowd that reacts to every move you make, so the ghost redraws worse each time you steer by it. Only this premise makes that danger, and only this premise is solved by standing still and letting him choose, which is the opposite of the pause film's answer, where you stopped the world to act. The twist lives in the brain, not the clock: you were never given a new sense, yours was turned up.
