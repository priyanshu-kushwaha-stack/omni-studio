# Claims ledger: What If You Fired Everyone and Hired AI? (G1)

Started at G1 (2026-11-15) for every real-world fact the packaging or the beat outline may state, so the claims check
can read the sources. G2 adds a row for every real-world claim the script speaks. c01-c06 were fetched and read by the
session itself (the arXiv abstract page and export.arxiv.org, 2026-11-15). c07-c28 were fetched and read on 2026-11-15
by a research pass (a cheaper-model subagent); the session re-fetched c08 (Keynes), c16 (Ford's five-dollar day) and
c17 (Ford's own words) and found each quote on the live page. Quotes are verbatim from the fetched text. Where a primary
page refused the fetch (403, Cloudflare, a cookie wall), the note says which copy was read instead.

The story is invented and carries no row: the app and its rule, you, the diner, the town, the plant across the road,
every person, every robot and every number inside the story.

**The paper.** Hemenway Falk and Tsoukalas (2026) is an arXiv preprint, revised three times, with no journal
reference: a theoretical model, not peer reviewed and not a measurement. Every row that rests on it is `contested`, and
the film says so in plain words ("two economists' model, this year, not yet checked by other economists").

**Corrections made in research** (never say the left side): "the robot that takes your job should pay taxes" is
Quartz's 2017 headline, not Bill Gates's words (c20); the robots-and-union-dues story was Walter Reuther's own, about an
unnamed Ford manager in Cleveland, not Henry Ford II (c18, contested); Baumol and Bowen wrote of a Schubert quartet, not
Beethoven (c13); OpenAI wrote "top 10%" on a simulated bar exam, and a 2024 study puts GPT-4 near the 62nd percentile of
first-time takers (c27, contested); the USDA table ends at 2000 (1.9%), so "under 2% today" becomes "by 2000, under 2%"
(c10); Keynes called technological unemployment "only a temporary phase of maladjustment" and is never quoted without it
(c07); the 2026 Alaska dividend is $1,200 including $200 of energy relief (c19); Allen's 46% against 12% is "one
widely cited estimate" (c15).
