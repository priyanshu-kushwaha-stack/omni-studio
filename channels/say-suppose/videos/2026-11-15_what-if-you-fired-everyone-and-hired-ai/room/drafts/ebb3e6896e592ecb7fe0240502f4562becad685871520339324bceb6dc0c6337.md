## Premise

You run a forty-stool diner across the road from a car plant. One Monday, with your cook off sick again, an app you never installed appears on your phone: type any job, and by the next shift a machine worker is at your door. No wage, no sleep, no mistakes. Hire as many as you like, rent them to anyone, and set what a shift costs with the app's one dial. At first, only you have it.

**The catch.** The workers you replace were your customers, and the workers everyone else replaces with your machines were your customers too. Each owner saves a whole wage and loses only a slice of the spending that wage carried, because the worker spent most of it at other people's counters; the rest of the loss lands on the neighbours. So every owner in town does the sum, gets it right, and automates anyway, because the one who stops first loses to the one who doesn't. Six Fridays in, the whistle blows and nobody crosses the road. The robots keep cooking for forty empty stools, and the plant that rents five hundred of your machines can't pay you, because nobody in town can buy a car. You own every machine in town and you are going broke.

**Why this setting.** A small car town: one plant, one road, one diner, so the trap fits in a single picture: the worker you fire ate across the road from where he worked, and so did the paint shop the plant fired with your machines. The ledger's best pictures and stories are American: Ford's five-dollar day and his own line about wages and sales (c16, c17), a union leader's story about a hall of machines at a car plant (c18), a burger robot's first day in Pasadena (c23), the bank tellers the ATM did not kill (c11), the farm share falling from forty-one percent to under two (c10), Alaska's yearly cheque (c19). The alternative weighed was a British mill town (the Luddites c26, Jevons c12, Keynes c07): it lost because the mill town's famous story is the workers smashing the machines, the worker's chair, and ours is the owner's chair; the Luddites still get their beat when the town comes for your robots. > DESIRE: To never again depend on a person showing up.
> CONFLICT: Every machine shift saves its owner a whole wage and costs the town a whole wage of spending, but only a slice lands on the owner who did it, so nobody stops, you least of all.

> RESULT: The whistle fills the stools again, you still collect on every machine that stays, and the plant boss is eating on stool four.
