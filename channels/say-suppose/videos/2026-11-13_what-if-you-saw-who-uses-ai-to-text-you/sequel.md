# Sequel: What If You Heard Who Uses AI to Call You?

## The hook (the last scene, no new rule)

The kitchen, the evening your boss typed his first message to you by hand and your father's "me to kid" landed before your hand left the phone. Your cousin abroad drops a voice note into the group chat, the first voice in a chat that has only ever been text. You press play. Her voice starts, mid-word, warm, and beside the little waveform something small and red begins to tick on. We cut before it settles. Part 1 said the rule covered "every message you can read"; it never said a word about voices. The viewer who comes back for Part 2 remembers one thing: a dot started to form beside a voice, and the film ended before you knew whether it finished.

## The rule twist, one line

The same dot, moved to sound: it ticks on beside any voice you hear through a speaker, a voice note, a call, a hold message, a video, whenever the voice was never in a throat.

## The opening skit (the owner's note, 2026-11-12)

Part 2 opens the way Part 1 does: a short acted skit before the narrator speaks. The kitchen, the same evening, from over your shoulder. The voice note plays to its end, your cousin saying she misses everyone and cannot wait for the homecoming, and the dot finishes ticking on. Your mother, at the sink: "Is that her? Play it again." You play it again. The dot stays. You press call. Your cousin answers on the second ring, her face in a window, her mouth moving, her voice the same voice, and beside the call, a tick. Your mother leans into frame, waving at her niece: "Look at you, so far away." Your face: brows high, eyes wide, mouth open, the stunned face from Part 1's thumbnail, and then the grin. The narrator: "That is your cousin. That is her face. And something in the room is telling you the voice isn't."

## Five beats for Part 2 (exciting by the same rules)

1. **Play it again.** The voice note, the call, the dot both times. Title question by 0:15: what if you could hear who uses AI to call you? The rule in three lines with its flagged limit: a dot says the voice was made, never whose words it is saying. Loophole closed in the scene: "just call them, then", and the call gets a dot too. Laugh: your mother tells the dotted voice "Look at you, so far away", and the voice says "I know, Tita".
2. **Every voice in the city.** The set pieces, each a use and each wider: the bank's hold voice, dot, obviously; your two aunts send each other sung birthday greetings, both dotted, both weeping with pride; Monday in your boss's office, he plays you a contract read aloud by his assistant's voice, dot, and you make him say the number out loud himself, the only line in the room without a dot; the dating man calls, bare, his own voice shaking, and you say yes again; the senator live on the morning radio, dot: he is not even in the studio, and you are the only listener in the city who knows. Loophole closed: "why not record it and prove it?" A recording of a dotted voice has no dot; the dot is on the listening, not the file.
3. **The call from the ship.** The restart at about three minutes, a narrow escape. A call comes to your mother's phone from your father's number, in your father's voice, asking her to send money for a port fee tonight. Dot. You take the phone from her hand before the transfer goes through and ask the voice to spell "birthday". It spells it right. You hang up. The family chat erupts; your mother, shaking: "It was his voice." You: a bubble, "It could spell." (Any real-world line about cloned-voice scams needs a new claims row; none is in the ledger, so none is spoken.)
4. **The Sunday calls.** The reveal at about 60%. Your father calls the kitchen every Sunday. Dot. Every Sunday. You go hunting and find the tablet in the cupboard: a voice built from his own recordings two winters ago, the month the ship lost its signal, trained by your mother so the kitchen would still hear him; she types what he texts her and lets the voice say it. His words. Her typing. A made voice. The dilemma at 75-80%, both sides plain: tell her you know, and the Sundays stop; keep quiet, and the one voice in the house you trusted is one you can hear is made. You act at once with the cleverest use in the film: you text him "call me" and set both phones on the table to see which rings first. Both ring. Your mother and your father, in two voices, say the same three words.
5. **The gangway.** The peak is the gag turned to sound, a triumph, wordless 8-10 s: the homecoming, the port, the gangway down; your mother in her best apron, your best friend holding the cake. He walks down. He says three words out loud, in a voice you have never heard, rough from eight months of wind, and gets one of them wrong. No speaker. No dot. The crowd roars. Then the twist and the cliffhanger, in the last tenth: on the drive home your mother plays the family the voice note you sent from the pier that morning, and beside your own voice, a dot. Your phone has been cleaning up your voice notes, and you never asked it to. Cut. Closing question: "So, how many of your voice notes has your phone tidied up? Tell us in the comments. And if you'd have done the same, hit like and subscribe."
