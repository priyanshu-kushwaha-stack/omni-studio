# Sequel: What If You Heard Who Uses AI to Call You?

## The hook (the last scene, no new rule)

The kitchen, the evening you sent your father three words and left the typo. His reply is still on the table, bare. Your cousin abroad drops a voice note into the group chat, the first voice in a chat that has only ever been text. You press play. Her voice starts, mid-word, warm, and beside the little waveform something small and red begins to tick on. We cut before it settles. Part 1 never said the rule covered voices; it said "every message you can read". The viewer who comes back for Part 2 remembers one thing: a dot started to form beside a voice, and the film ended before you knew whether it finished.

## The rule twist, one line

The same dot, moved to sound: it ticks on beside any voice you hear through a speaker, a voice note, a call, a hold message, a video, whenever the voice was never in a throat.

## The opening skit (the owner's note, 2026-11-12)

Part 2 opens the way Part 1 does: a short acted skit before the narrator speaks. The kitchen, the same evening, from over your shoulder. The voice note plays to its end, your cousin saying she misses everyone and cannot wait for the homecoming, and the dot finishes ticking on. Your mother, at the sink: "Is that her? Play it again." You play it again. The dot stays. You press call. Your cousin answers on the second ring, her face in a window, her mouth moving, her voice the same voice, and beside the call, a tick. Your mother leans into frame, waving at her niece: "Look at you, so far away." You look at the dot. The narrator: "That is your cousin. That is her face. And something in the room is telling you that the voice is not."

## Five beats for Part 2

1. **Play it again.** The voice note, the call, the dot both times. Your habit from Part 1 is new and fragile: you have just learned to trust three bare words over a paragraph. The twist hits exactly that, because a voice was the one thing you still took on trust. Title question by 0:15: what if you could hear who uses AI to call you? The rule in three lines, with its flagged limit: a dot says the voice was made, never whose words it is saying.
2. **Every voice in the city.** The montage turns at each use. The bank's hold message, dot, obviously. The man from the dating app, unblocked, leaves you a voice note, and it is bare: his own voice, shaking. Your boss's cheerful all-hands recording, dot. Your two aunts send each other sung birthday greetings, both dotted, both delighted. A call from your best friend at four in the morning, her voice thick with sleep, bare. Then your mother plays you the voicemail your father left on her birthday last month, the one she has saved and replayed every night. Dot. The gap: the one voice in the house nobody has doubted.
3. **The ship.** Your father, eight months out, calls the kitchen every Sunday. Dot, every Sunday. You say nothing to your mother. You start listening for the seams, the breaths that come at the wrong place, the words he never used. You find none, and that frightens you more. Problem two: a voice that is never wrong in a mouth that is never there. Open loop: the homecoming is in two weeks, and the man walking off the ship will have a voice you have never heard with a dot beside it or without.
4. **The saved voicemail.** The cost, at about 60%. You find the app on your father's old tablet in the cupboard, under the shoebox: a voice built from his own recordings, trained by your mother, two years ago, the winter the ship lost its signal for a month and he could not call. She has been making his Sunday calls herself, typing what he texts her and letting the voice say it, so that the kitchen still hears him. His words. Her typing. A made voice. Dilemma at 75%: tell her you know, and the Sundays stop; or sit at the table every Sunday and let a voice you can hear is made keep the house warm.
5. **The gangway.** The pay-off is the gag turned to sound. The homecoming, the port, the gangway down; your mother in her best apron and your best friend holding the cake. He walks down. He says three words, out loud, in a voice you have never heard, rough from eight months of wind, and he gets one of them wrong. No dot, because there is no speaker; there is a man. Your mother laughs: "He never could say it either." The residue, inside the last image of the phone face down on the kitchen table: tomorrow a voice will tell you it misses you, and you will not be able to hear who typed it, and you will have to decide, the way she did, whether that is the question.
