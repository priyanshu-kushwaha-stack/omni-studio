# Claims ledger: What If You Saw Who Uses AI to Text You? (G1)

Started at G1 (2026-11-13) for every real-world fact the packaging or the beat outline may state, so the claims check
can read the sources. G2 adds a row for every real-world claim the script speaks. Sources were fetched and read on
2026-11-13 by three research passes; c01, c04 (the overseas-worker count) and c20 were re-fetched by the session itself. Quotes are
verbatim from the fetched text. Where a primary page refused the fetch (403 or a timeout), the note says which page was
read instead.

The story is invented and carries no row: the dot and the rule, you, every message, every person and every number
inside the story (forty-one messages, thirty-three dots).

**The world.** The brief's world rests on "Manila, the texting capital of the world". The claim holds for the
**country**, not the city: Philstar (2001) and Rappler (2014) call the Philippines the texting capital of the world
(c01). The film may be set in Manila, but it says "the Philippines" when it states the nickname, and "was called",
never "is". The brief's "hundreds of millions of texts a day" is wrong in both directions: about thirty million a day
in 2001, over a billion a day by 2008 (c02).

**Dropped from the topic brief or the research.**
- "Manila, the texting capital of the world": the sources give the nickname to the country (c01 is the kept version).
- "Hundreds of millions of SMS a day" and the "400 million a day" figure, which appears only on blogs (c02 is the kept version).
- "Typos, informality or handwriting make a message seem warmer or more authentic": no study found. The film's turn
  (the typo as a signature) is the story's idea, never said as a finding. Zahavi (c13) is the writer's lens for it,
  and the effort heuristic (c12) is the nearest real result.
- "Weizenbaum's secretary asked him to leave so she could be alone with it": the "alone" wording is a later spoken
  recollection, not the book (c08 is the kept version).
- "Pew: 23% have used AI for personal writing": not found at Pew. Pew measures ChatGPT use only (c26).
- Match/Kinsey "Singles in America" 2025 figures (26% of singles use AI in dating, 10% for first messages): the page
  refused the fetch. The 2024 figures are kept (c27).
- Imperva "51% of traffic is bots" as "first time ever": Imperva says "for the first time in a decade" (c09).
- Ifverse "closest video" URL: not a claim in the film. `omni similar` covers the overlap at G1.
