# Research: how companies already predict what people do

Film: "What If AI Predicted Every Choice You Make?" (Say Suppose). Fetch date: 2026-11-13.

Status key:
- **sourced**: I read the quote in the source myself (page fetched, or PDF text pulled out).
- **sourced (reprint)**: the original page blocked the fetch (403). I read the quote in a full reprint, and search-index excerpts of the original match it word for word.
- **disputed**: the source really says it, but credible people question it.
- **unverified**: I could not read a primary source.

## (b) Do not say

- "Target's AI knew a teenager was pregnant before her father did." The story comes from one unnamed employee, has never been confirmed, and Eric Siegel calls it "essentially been debunked." If you use it, frame it as a famous story that may not be true.
- "Target's model was 87% accurate." The 87% is from a made-up example shopper, "Jenny Ward."
- "Uber charges more when your battery is low." Uber's economist said the opposite, while admitting the correlation.
- "Amazon ships things before you order them." It's a patent, and use was never confirmed (a 2023 retrospective says it appears it was not put into action).
- "Netflix never used anything from the prize" or "Netflix threw away the $1M algorithm." It used two Progress Prize algorithms and skipped the final ensemble.
- "Netflix picks 80% of what you watch." The paper says recommendations *influence* choice for about 80% of hours streamed. It's a 2015 figure reported by Netflix itself.
- "75% of YouTube viewing comes from recommendations." That was CNET's pre-correction figure, still in the CBS reprint. The corrected figure is "more than 70 percent" (2018).
- "BellKor won by one second," or "The Ensemble had the better score." They tied on the hidden test set, and the earlier entry, about 20 minutes earlier (sources say 15-24 minutes), won.
- "Researchers unmasked 99% of Netflix users." The 99% figure is for records in the released dataset, under stated conditions (8 ratings, 2 may be wrong, dates within 14 days).
- "Prediction markets always beat polls." The finding is 74% of 964 poll comparisons, US presidential vote share, 1988-2004, Iowa Electronic Markets.
- "The Pentagon's terror market predicted attacks." It never opened.
- "The best AI got the kids' grades 20% right." R² of about 0.2 is variance explained, not a hit rate. Don't quote 0.23 or 0.19 for named outcomes unless someone checks Fig. 3 by eye.
- "AI can't predict anything about people." Salganik et al. claim "practical limits to the predictability of life outcomes in some settings."
- Any Smart Compose "1 billion" or "11%" figure. Only the 2 billion characters a week (Pichai, 2019) is verified.
