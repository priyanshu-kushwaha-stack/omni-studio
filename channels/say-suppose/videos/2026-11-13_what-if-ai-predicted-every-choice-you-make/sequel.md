# Sequel: What If AI Couldn't Predict One Person?

## The hook (the last scene, no new rule)

The train, a month after the key. Every head on its phone, every card teal, and two crimson cards in the middle of the bench, yours and your best friend's, neither of you looking up. Across the aisle the kid with the cap, feet swinging, no phone. Over his head a cream card with nothing on it. Not crimson, not teal: blank, the thing the city made once at the counter and never again. He catches you looking, and winks. Part 1 planted the blank card as the limit made visible: the machine has no card for a choice nobody has made before. The viewer who comes back for Part 2 remembers one thing: in a city where everyone else is a timetable, one kid rides the train every morning with no forecast at all, and he knows it.

## The rule twist, one line

The same tags over everyone, but the kid's card never prints, and whoever stands within arm's reach of him goes blank too, so the city's whole price list follows him around like weather.

## The opening skit (the owner's note, 2026-11-12)

Part 2 opens the way Part 1 does: a short acted skit before the narrator speaks. The same train, the same bench, from the kid's side of the aisle. He gets up, crosses, and sits down between you and your best friend without asking. A paper flap, then silence: your crimson card flaps once and goes blank. His too. The fare board at the end of the coach stops ticking and shows nothing. The passenger on your other side looks up at her own card, LOOKS AT PHONE 99%, then at the three blanks beside her, and moves two seats down. The kid takes a sweet out of his pocket, looks at it, and puts it in your best friend's apron pocket instead of his own mouth. Your best friend: "Why'd you do that?" Kid: "Dunno yet." Then the narrator.

## Five beats for Part 2

1. **The bench.** Three blank cards on one train, and a fare board with nothing on it. Your habit, planted in Part 1, is reading the card before you reach; the change hits exactly that: beside him there is nothing to read, and your hand has forgotten how to move first. Title question by 0:30: what if the AI couldn't predict one person, and standing next to him made it blind to you?
2. **Everyone wants him, and nobody wants him near.** The montage turns at every use. The magician hires him to stand at the table, and the act is back: PALMS THE QUEEN never prints. The man with the same lunch finds him at the next table and his card goes blank for the first time in a year; he can't eat. The coffee seller serves the kid for free because her board can't price him, then charges you double because yours has gone quiet too. The landlord's letter has no number on it. The gap every time: you expect blank to mean free, and the city prices nothing as the most expensive thing there is.
3. **The city makes an offer.** Halfway, the rate board on the lamppost grows a second line: a discount for being unpredictable. The coach on the street changes his chant to "Never the same. Never the same." Queues form to do something new, and within a week the new things are all the same new things, and the cards print again, teal, ninety-nine. The kid's stays blank. Open loop: the only person the city can't buy is the one who isn't trying.
4. **The sister.** The cost, at 60%. Your best friend's sister, the voice on the phone in Part 1, comes to the stall with the people he owes a step behind her; their cards read the same thing over all three heads, and the kid is on the train, not here. The city reads them perfectly and does nothing, because a forecast is not a trial either way. Dilemma at 75%: fetch the kid, and stand him between her and them so nobody's card can say what happens next; or trust the key in your best friend's pocket and the two of you with no shield at all.
5. **The wink.** The pay-off is the hand turned round again. You stay. Your best friend holds out his hand, and you drop the key into it, and he hands the box to the men and the men take nothing, because the whole court is watching two crimson cards and a man who just gave them what they came for. Then the kid walks in, late, with a sweet for each of them. Nothing over his head. The residue, inside the last image of the empty cream card on a thread: tomorrow someone will do a small thing nobody asked for, and for one second you'll have no idea what they'll do next, and that second is the only one that's yours.
