# Sequel: What If AI Couldn't Predict One Person?

## The hook (the last scene, no new rule)

The morning after the queue. Every head on the train on its phone, every card teal, and two crimson cards in the middle of the bench, yours and your best friend's, the key dropped into his palm without a glance up. The city already has a card for the trick: by lunch a thousand people held out keys and HANDS OVER KEY printed teal over every one of them, and the newsreader said confidence was back to ninety-nine. Across the aisle the kid with the cap, feet swinging, no phone, and over his head a cream card with nothing on it. Not crimson. Not teal. Nothing. He winks. Part 1 planted the blank card as the limit made visible, and then showed the city closing that limit in a day. The viewer who comes back for Part 2 remembers one thing: in a city that can copy any trick by lunch, one kid rides the train every morning with no forecast at all, and the city hasn't noticed yet.

## The rule twist, one line

The same tags over everyone, but the kid's card never prints, and whoever stands within arm's reach of him goes blank too, so the city's whole price list follows him around like weather.

## The opening skit (the owner's note, 2026-11-12)

Part 2 opens the way Part 1 does: a short acted skit before the narrator speaks, the title after it. The same train, the same bench, from the kid's side of the aisle. He gets up, crosses, and sits down between you and your best friend without asking. A paper flap, then silence: your crimson card flaps once and goes blank. His too. The fare board at the end of the coach stops ticking and shows nothing. The passenger on your other side looks up at her own card, LOOKS AT PHONE 99%, then at the three blanks beside her, and moves two seats down. Your best friend holds out his hand, palm up, the grin already there. Friend: "Trust me?" The kid takes a sweet from his pocket and drops it in the palm. Friend: "That's not the key." Kid: "Isn't it?" Then the narrator, and the title question by 0:15: what if the AI couldn't predict one person, and standing next to him made it blind to you?

## Five beats for Part 2

1. **The bench.** Three blank cards on one train, and a fare board with nothing on it. Your habit from Part 1 is reading the card before you reach; beside him there is nothing to read, and your hand has forgotten how to move first. The coffee seller can't price him and pours anyway; the kid pays with the sweet. Stakes by 0:30: blank means the city can't bill you, and nobody knows yet whether that is free or the most expensive thing there is.
2. **Everyone wants him near, and nobody wants him close.** The set pieces climb. The magician hires him to stand at the table and PALMS THE QUEEN never prints: the act is back. The man with the same lunch finds him at the next table, goes blank for the first time in a year, and can't eat. The copycat queue from Part 1 leaves your stall and follows the kid down the street, and every card in it goes blank and every board beside it shows nothing. The gap each time: you expect blank to mean free, and the city prices nothing as the highest rate it has. Laugh: the coach changes his chant to "Never the same. Never the same." and the queue in front of him prints LISTENS 99%.
3. **The city makes an offer.** The rate board on the lamppost grows a second line, a bounty, rat tails again: a month of half price for whoever makes the kid's card print. The newsreader reads it out. The whole city tries. Dares, pranks, a brass band on the platform: his card stays cream, and every new thing the city does to him prints teal over the doer within a day. Loophole closed on screen: "Why not just ask him what he'll do next?" You do. Kid: "Dunno yet." The board flickers and shows nothing.
4. **The cost, at 60%.** The city finds the pattern it can read: wherever the kid stands, the city's score drops into the eighties, so it bills the space around him. Fares triple for a coach he is in; the court's boards climb when he sits down; the landlord's letter has no number on it, and the coffee seller's shutter comes down when he walks in. The copycats stop copying and start keeping their distance, then keeping you at a distance, because you are the one he sits next to. Dilemma at 75%: take the bounty, which the city will pay the moment the kid does the one thing it can already print, the key trick, now a teal card for anyone who holds a key out; or sit him at your counter every day and let the whole court go blank and full price with you.
5. **The wink.** The pay-off is the running gag turned round once more. Closing, the court watching, the newsreader's crew at the door. You hold the key out across the box to the kid. "Trust me?" He takes it, unlocks the box, and drops his sweet in with the coins. His card flaps for the first time, and prints, and what it prints is a question mark. Then every card in the court flaps to the same mark, then every card on every lamppost to the horizon, a thousand cream cards turning at once like snow, the mind-boggling image, and the city's board on the pillar shows nothing at all. Triumph: your best friend, under a question mark, holds out his hand, and you drop the key into it. The residue, inside the last image of the kid's cap on the counter and a cream card on a thread above an empty chair: tomorrow someone will do a small thing nobody asked for, and for one second you will have no idea what they'll do next, and that second is the only one that's yours. The city's board behind it flaps once and shows a single teal tick. It has started learning him.
