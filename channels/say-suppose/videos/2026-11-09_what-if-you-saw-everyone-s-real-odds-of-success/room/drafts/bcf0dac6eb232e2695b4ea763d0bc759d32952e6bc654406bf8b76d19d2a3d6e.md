# Script: What If You Saw Everyone's Real Odds of Success?

format: story
status: v1, the G2 room's first draft (2026-11-09), from the G1 round-3 beat outline and its open notes
claims: claims.md

> SITUATION: You sit in a night batch for UPSC's civil services exam, copying the topper's notes, sure that hard work and the right notes make anything possible
> DESIRE: To get in, and to know this year will pay off before you spend another
> CONFLICT: Your chip reads zero point zero four per cent, so you chase the biggest number you can see and steer everyone, your cousin Aarav first, towards a safe one
> CHANGE: Your advice drains Aarav's chip to zero, and you learn that a number tells you what is likely, never what is worth choosing
> RESULT: You know your odds and work from your own notes anyway; Aarav is back in the lane with his bat

## [00:00] The change
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: A coaching hall at night under tube lights, rows of desks, a ceiling fan turning. Row three: you, bent over, copying the topper's notebook from the desk beside you, line for line. The thumbnail's set and pose from the first frame: up and to the right of your head, where every chip sits, your crimson chip is already lit. Tags are stamped beside a chip, never over a face.
> HOLD: 0.2 s. A pen scratch and the tube-light hum on the first frame; the first word comes at once.
Every night, the topper's notes. Then yours: zero point zero four per cent.
> SCENE: On "Then yours", close on your face, the thumbnail's pose: eyes wide, brows up, pen stopped mid-word. Behind you, with a soft flutter like split-flap cards, small chips flicker on over the other heads, row by row, warm white and unlabelled. Numerals show only on the key chips, never on the crowd.
> TAG: 0.04%
What if you saw everyone's real odds of success?

## [00:20] The rule
> SCENE: The camera pulls up out of row three, through the ceiling. The hall becomes a code-drawn grid of small paper chairs, all dark but one, lit crimson.
Zero point zero four. What's that, in people?
One chair. Out of two thousand five hundred.
> HOLD: 2 s. The grid hangs; the one crimson chair pulses once; the camera starts back down.
> SCENE: Back down to your face. Your eyes go to the fee receipt tucked in your notebook, then to the wall clock.
A year of fees. A year of your life. On one chair. So... keep going?
> SCENE: Close on the teacher's chip, then the topper's: each number flutters as its owner turns a page.
Here's how it works. Each chip is one person's chance at the one thing they'd choose... with nobody watching. It never tells you what that thing is.
It starts where the crowd starts. Then it moves with what they do.
> SCENE: You look round the hall at the numbers, chin on your fist, and nod to yourself.
And you decide the way you decide everything. Find the biggest number. Copy that.
The biggest number is the right choice.
> HOLD: 3 s. The ident: the title card (C) slides in on the channel's four-note signal while the camera drifts across the hall's glowing chips behind it.

## [01:07] The first jump: the lane and the hall
> MOOD: dusk
> SCENE: The lane outside home, dusk. Your cousin Aarav, eleven, in a cap, takes guard with a taped bat against a chalk wicket on the wall. You bowl.
Your cousin Aarav. Eleven. Talks about cricket every single day.
So you're expecting a huge number.
> SCENE: Aarav's chip flickers on above his cap. Close on your face: surprised.
> TAG: 0.2%
Zero point two per cent.
> SCENE: You walk over, turn his hands round on the handle, step back. You bowl. He hits it into the wall and grins.
> BUBBLE: Aarav: "One more over!"
You fix his grip anyway. One more over. Another tomorrow. You promise.
> HOLD: 2.5 s. He hits one cleanly, a crisp thwack, and points the bat at you, grinning. The light goes.
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: The hall, the next night. The teacher at the board, arms wide, booming at the batch. His chip flickers on.
But Aarav's tiny number follows you into the hall. So you look for certainty. The teacher.
> TAG: 3%
Three per cent.
> BUBBLE: Teacher: "Rank guaranteed!"
> SCENE: You blink at him. He winks at the batch.
Okay. The topper, then. Surely.
> SCENE: The topper beside you, writing fast. Her chip flickers on. Close on your face: sceptical, then worried. You slowly push her notebook back to her.
> TAG: 8%
Eight per cent. The notes you copy all year... at eight.
> MOOD: day
> SCENE: The break. Outside, the chai stall under the coaching centre's sign; the chaiwala pours from a height. Far back by the kettle, a figure in a long dark coat with a crimson head, and no chip over him at all.
So, at the break: the chaiwala. Expecting another fantasy.
> TAG: 61%
Sixty-one per cent.
> SCENE: You stare at his chip, then at your own crimson one, then at his again.
Sixty-one! The biggest number on the street.
Mock test on Sunday... or the stall?
> SCENE: You fold your scarf into your bag and tie on an apron. You hold out your hand for the kettle.
> BUBBLE: Chaiwala: "You? Here?"
The stall. Stand where he stands, and his number's yours. Right?
> HOLD: 2.5 s. Montage, under a second each: you pour tea onto your own shoe; you count change wrong; you nap on a sack of tea leaves. Above, in the coaching centre's lit window, the batch goes on without you.

## [02:07] The second jump: the month
> SCENE: A wall calendar flips one month. Close on your crimson chip as its cards flutter down.
> TAG: 0.02%
A month at the stall. You miss the mock. And your chip doesn't climb to sixty-one. It drops.
> SCENE: The chaiwala's chip turns over like a card. On its back, a stamped label.
> TAG: SECOND STALL
His sixty-one was never about tea. It's a second stall. His goal.
You can't borrow someone's odds. Only their apron.
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: The hall at night, empty but for you. You sit in row three, apron still on. Your small crimson chip. The camera pulls back and the hall becomes the chair grid from the opening, re-lit white and stretching to the horizon: everyone who applied, every chip the same dim white.
Your chip started from a crowd too.
Last year? About one in a thousand UPSC civil services applicants got in (c01).
> HOLD: 2 s. The camera drifts along the endless rows and settles back on you: the one crimson chip among them, sitting lower than the rest.
You've been wearing the crowd's number. Like a verdict.
But yours moved. Down.
Copying their notes was never going to copy their number.
> SCENE: Back in the empty hall. You untie the apron, take your scarf from your bag, and look at the door, then at your notes.
So. Back to the kettle, or back to row three?
> SCENE: You pull your own notebook out of the bag. A blank first page. You start writing. Your chip's cards flutter.
> TAG: 0.03%
Row three. Your own notes this time. And the chip... stirs.
> SCENE: On the coaching centre's notice board behind you, among the timetables, a small stamped paper tag that says SUBSCRIBE. No sound.

## [02:59] The cost
> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: Home, evening, one lamp. Aarav at your door with his taped bat and a folded paper: the cricket academy's fee slip.
At home, Aarav's waiting. A fee slip for a cricket academy. And one question. For you. The one who can see the numbers.
> BUBBLE: Aarav: "Am I good?"
> SCENE: Over your shoulder: his chip, warm white.
> TAG: 0.2%
Your own number moves. You've seen it. His... you just read.
> SCENE: Close on the fee slip in Aarav's hands. Then a close on your face. Then a slow push on the lamp.
Zero point two. Against the family's money. And the biggest number is the right choice. Isn't it?
> SCENE: You crouch to his height and put a hand on his shoulder.
Quit.

His fingers open on the handle (2 s). The bat tips, rolls across the floor in slow motion and under the cupboard, out of sight (2 s). The lamp stays.
> TAG: 0%
> SCENE: Aarav walks to his room. Close on the dark gap under the cupboard.
He didn't even argue.

## [03:38] The third jump: the city
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: The last bus home, your forehead against the window. Outside, the city passes, and over every head in it a chip: thousands, flickering, each one changing as its person moves.
The last bus home. And you can't stop reading them. Like timetables.
> HOLD: 2.5 s. The bus rolls through streets of chips, no numerals: a woman jogging, her chip brightening with each stride; a boy asleep on a shop counter, his dimming.
> SCENE: The bus slows past a lit window: the teacher at his kitchen table, filling in an exam form. His chip turns over like a card.
> TAG: HIS OWN RANK
The teacher? He's sitting the exam himself. Again. His three was never about you.
> SCENE: Another window: the topper at a keyboard, playing. Her chip turns over, then ticks up.
> TAG: MUSIC
Her eight was music, all along. A year of notes... copied from someone who doesn't want the exam.
> SCENE: A blink-long cut as the bus passes: the chai stall (the stall layers from before, plus a half-down shutter), the chaiwala dropping coins into a tin with a drawing of a second stall on it. His chip ticks up.
> SCENE: The bus turns into your lane. Aarav's window, dark. A grey chip.
And Aarav's.
> HOLD: 2 s. The bus stops. A slow push towards the grey chip in the dark window. One note.
> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: Home. You kneel in front of the cupboard and look into the dark gap. In its glass door, your head and chip sprite flipped at about a quarter strength (cut it if it costs the week).
So. Leave the bat where it is, and keep following the biggest number?
Or give it back... and let him choose?
> SCENE: Your hand goes straight into the dark gap.
The biggest number? It tells you what's likely. It can't tell you what's worth choosing.
> SCENE: You pull out the bat and knock on Aarav's door.
You give it back.
> HOLD: 4 s. Aarav takes the bat in both hands, looks at it, and runs for the door. His chip flickers, grey to warm white.
> TAG: 0.2%
Zero point two. Same as before.
But it's his. And it's moving.
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: The lane, under one street lamp. Aarav at the chalk wicket, bat up, the tennis ball held out to you.
> BUBBLE: Aarav: "One more over!"
You promised.
> HOLD: 3 s. You bowl. He swings and misses, laughs, and points the bat at you for another. His chip flutters.

## The viewer's "but what about"
Answered where they arise:
1. What counts as success, who decides? The rule (0:15): the one thing they'd choose with nobody watching.
2. What does zero point zero four per cent mean in people? The rule: one chair out of two thousand five hundred.
3. Does it know my effort, does it change if I study? The rule says it moves with what they do; your copying put
   it below the crowd's one in a thousand, the month at the stall drops it further and your own notes stir it.
4. Can I borrow someone else's odds? Answered only after the stall fails: "Only their apron." (retention editor,
   G1 round 3).
5. Is my cousin's dream mine to judge? The cost and the choice: you judge it, his chip drains, you give the bat back.
