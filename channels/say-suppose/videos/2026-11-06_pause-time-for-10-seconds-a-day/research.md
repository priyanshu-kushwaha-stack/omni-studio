# Research: pause time for 10 seconds a day

Fetched and read on 2026-11-06. Every spoken claim, its exact quote and its source are in `claims.md`; each quote
was checked against the saved page text. This file is the reading behind them.

## What the sources say

- **Stopped time is physically impossible to survive as told.** Physicist Sean Carroll (Caltech, via Live
  Science): frozen air would trap you, and light would no longer reach your eyes. The script names both and cheats
  on exactly those two.
- **What ten seconds holds.** A one-metre fall takes about 0.45 s (OpenStax; checked against NIST g). People walk
  0.94 to 1.43 m/s by age and sex (Bohannon & Andrews 2011). The 100 m record is 9.58 s (World Athletics). Average
  typing 52 wpm (Cambridge, CHI 2018); silent reading 238 wpm for non-fiction (Brysbaert 2019); resting heart 60 to
  100 bpm (MedlinePlus).
- **Your brain already stretches time.** Chronostasis, the stopped-clock illusion, happens on every eye movement
  (Yarrow et al., Nature 2001).
- **Roads.** About 1.16 million road deaths a year; the leading cause of death at ages 5 to 29 (WHO, July 2026).
  Road design allows 1.5 s to perceive and 2.5 s total before braking (AASHTO via the Los Angeles street design
  manual). A car at 48 km/h needs about 23 m to stop (UK Highway Code). No bus figure was found.
- **Time really does run at different rates.** ISS crews return 0.007 s younger after six months (ESA). Your head
  ages about 90 billionths of a second faster than your feet over 79 years (NIST, Chou et al. 2010). GPS clocks gain
  about 38 microseconds a day; ignored, positions drift about 10 km a day (Pogge, Ohio State).
