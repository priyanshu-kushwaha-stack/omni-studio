# Script: What If You Could Pause Time for 10 Seconds a Day?

## [00:00] The change
> MOOD: frozen
> SCENE: Flash-forward. A grey, frozen street at dusk. A bus a metre from a school-age kid on a crossing. The split-flap counter reads 7. Letterbox bars. Cut to black.
> HOLD: 2 s. One low note, then silence.
On day twelve, all that stands between a bus and a kid is ten seconds.
> MOOD: morning
> SCENE: Bedroom, morning light through the curtain. The alarm clock rings. You snap your fingers at it, and the room drains to grey from your hand outward. Bars slide in. Dust hangs in the light.
But on day one, you just wake up and discover you can pause time.
The alarm's frozen mid-ring. Your cat's stuck halfway off the bed.
So what if you could pause time for ten seconds a day?
> SCENE: Colour floods back, bars slide out. The cat lands. The alarm finishes its ring.

## [00:20] The rule
> SCENE: The split-flap counter drops in, top right, reading 10. You snap; the bedroom goes grey.
Ten seconds. Once a day. Everyone and everything stops. Except you.
> SCENE: The counter flips down to 0. Colour snaps back; the cat finishes its jump.
When the ten run out, the world starts again, and nobody knows.
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: A bedside clock turns to midnight. The counter's flaps blank, then flip back to 10.
And unused seconds vanish at midnight.
> MOOD: frozen
> SCENE: Grey. Your breath stirs the frozen air. Your eyes catch the light. You lift a pillow, let go, and it hangs.
So we cheat. Air moves for you. You can see. And anything you touch moves with you, then stops dead when you let go.
> SCENE: You hold up one finger, thinking.
So, what's your first pause for?
Something selfish. Obviously.

## [00:50] The first days
> MOOD: day
> SCENE: Kitchen. Your phone slips out of your hand, screen-first towards the tiles.
> TAG: 0.45 S
A phone falls a metre in about 0.45 seconds (c04). Usually, you just watch it go.
Not today.
> SCENE: You snap. Grey, bars in. The phone hangs a hand's width above the floor. The camera pulls out side-on through the walls; a paper tape runs past the rooms as you walk.
You stroll over. There's no rush. At an ordinary walking pace, ten seconds covers about 10 to 14 metres (c05).
> TAG: 10–14 M
> SCENE: Close on your hand plucking the phone out of the air. Colour, bars out. You check the screen: not a crack. A smirk.
Not a crack.
> SCENE: Hallway at work. Your boss steps out of an office, waving.
> BUBBLE: Boss: "Got a minu—"
Your boss spots you in the hallway. Stop and chat, or vanish?
> SCENE: You snap. Grey. The boss frozen mid-wave. You tiptoe past, under his arm.
Vanish.
> SCENE: Colour. The boss blinks at the empty hallway.
> SCENE: Office kitchen. One samosa left on the plate. A colleague's hand reaches for it.
The last samosa. A colleague's hand is already reaching. Do you let it go?
> SCENE: You snap. Grey. You swap the samosa for a napkin and stroll off chewing. Colour. The colleague stares at the napkin.
Of course not. You're not a saint. You're a person with ten seconds.
> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: Home, evening, one warm lamp. Your mother's chappal flies across the room at your head. You snap. Grey. It hangs mid-air. You step one pace left. Colour. It sails past. She stares at her hand, then laughs.
Your mother's chappal, mid-air, aimed at your head? You step aside. She never works out how she missed.
> MOOD: day
> SCENE: Close on the hallway wall clock. Your eyes dart to it. The second hand sticks for a beat, then jumps.
Have you ever glanced at a clock and the second hand seemed to stick for a moment?
> TAG: CHRONOSTASIS
That's real. Scientists call it chronostasis (c09).
> SCENE: A paper TV remote drops into your hand.
Your brain has been editing time for you all your life. Now you've got the remote.

## [02:30] The first year
> MOOD: dusk
> SCENE: Street at dusk. Crowds, pigeons, a zebra crossing. A bus comes down the road.
> TAG: DAY 12
Then comes day twelve. You're walking home.
A bus runs a red light.
> SCENE: A school-age kid in a blue cap chases a ball onto the crossing. Close on your face: eyes wide, brows up.
There's a kid in the road.
> SCENE: Wide, side-on. A paper tape measure unrolls from the moving bus's bumper towards the kid, and runs past them.
> TAG: 23 M
A car at 48 kilometres an hour needs about 23 metres to stop (c14). And this is a bus.
Do you shout? Do you run?
Neither works. Not in time.
> SCENE: Far back in the crowd, a figure in a long dark coat, with a crimson head, stands still, facing the wrong way. Nobody looks at them.
You hit pause.
> MOOD: frozen
> SCENE: The world drains to grey from you outward; bars slide in. The bus stops a metre from the kid. Pigeons hang mid-flap. The counter drops to 10.
You run.
> SCENE: You sprint into the road, scarf streaming, motion blur. The counter falls on each word.
Nine. Eight. Seven.
> SCENE: You lift the frozen kid off the crossing and set them down on the pavement. The ball stays in the road.
Six. Five. Four.
> SCENE: You step back onto the kerb, breathing hard.
Three. Two. One.
> MOOD: dusk
> SCENE: Colour returns, bars out. The bus brakes, horn blaring, through the crossing, and flattens the ball. The kid blinks at the pavement.
The bus screams through an empty crossing. The ball doesn't make it. The kid does.
> SCENE: The kid's mother runs over and hugs the kid, then turns to thank the shaken bus driver. You stand at the edge of the frame.
The kid's mother runs over, shaking. She thanks the bus driver. Nobody thanks you. Nobody knows to.
> HOLD: 2.5 s. You walk away down the street, hands in pockets, trembling. The music settles into the theme for you.

## [03:30] The catch
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: Night. Close on your hands, still trembling. Then your face in the dark. Then you at the window, looking down at the street.
You saved a life with ten seconds. That feels free.
It is not free.
Your body keeps ageing through every pause. Ten seconds a day that nobody else lives.
And now you know what ten seconds can do. So you can't stop watching.
> MOOD: morning
> SCENE: Morning. The bus stop. The bus doors start to close. Your fingers twitch.
One morning you're late, and the bus doors are closing. Catch it, or keep your seconds?
> SCENE: You snap. Grey. You stroll aboard. Colour. You sit down, pleased with yourself.
Just this once.
> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: That evening, home, one lamp. Your mother at the top of the stairs, carrying a basket of washing. She misses a step. You snap. Nothing: the counter reads 0.
That evening, your mother misses a step on the stairs. You snap your fingers. Nothing.
> HOLD: 4.5 s. Slow motion; the warm lamp stays, the shadows deepen and the vignette closes in (no grey: the pause failed). She falls the last three steps. The music drops to a single held note, then nothing. You kneel beside her. She sits up, rubbing her arm, and waves you off.
She's all right. Bruised. You're not.
> MOOD: night
> SCENE: Night. You in bed, eyes open, the counter on the wall reading 10.
That night, you lie awake doing the maths of every second you've ever wasted.
> TAG: MONTH 6
> SCENE: Montage, two seconds each, the counter top right reading 10: you at the crossing, watching. You on the bus, watching. You at the foot of the stairs, watching your mother come down.
You almost never spend them on yourself again.
> SCENE: Bedroom, midnight. The counter still reads 10. You lie awake, then snap. Grey. The city outside the window hangs still.
Some days nothing happens. So at midnight you spend them on nothing at all.
> HOLD: 2 s. The frozen city, just for you. A held chord.
Ten seconds of a city holding its breath, just for you.
> SCENE: Across the street, in a frozen grey building, a curtain in one window swings shut. The counter flips to 0. Colour. You stare at the window.
Except, across the street, a curtain moves. In a window that should be frozen.

## [04:30] The long run
> SCENE: You at your window, night after night, watching the building across the street. The curtain never moves.
The curtain never moves again. But every midnight, you check.
> MOOD: day
> SCENE: Street cricket in a lane. A ball skied high towards the boundary wall. You snap. Grey. You walk under the frozen ball, hands cupped. Colour. It drops into your hands. The lane erupts.
Catch the six that wins your lane's cricket match? That, you do. Just the once.
Ten seconds is too small for greed. It's exactly the right size for an accident.
> SCENE: Quick cuts, two seconds each: a bottle you nudge from under a bike wheel; a pan handle you turn inward; a toddler you lift back from the top stair.
You become the reason small disasters don't happen.
> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: Home, one lamp. Your mother, older now, watching you watch her on the stairs. Close on her raised brows.
Your mother asks you to stop waiting at the bottom of the stairs.
Promise her, or keep watching?
> SCENE: You stay on the bottom step. She shakes her head, and smiles, and takes your hand on the way down.
You stay on the bottom step.
> MOOD: dusk
> TAG: YEAR 40
> HOLD: 4 s. The same street through the seasons: leaves, rain, snow, sun. You age a little in each. In one frozen winter frame, far back, the figure in the long dark coat stands among the grey crowd, facing you.
> SCENE: You, older, grey in the tuft, at a corner. Your eyes flick to a wobbling ladder, a loose dog, a driver looking at a phone.
Forty years in, you read a street like a weather map.
You see the accident before it happens. Most days, you don't even need the pause.
> SCENE: Two paper clocks side by side. Yours gives a small extra tick every day.
Your body lives every one of those pauses. Over fifty years, they add up to about two days.
> MOOD: lamp
> SCENE: A mirror. You squint at your reflection. Beside it, not on your face, a tag.
> TAG: 2 DAYS
That's how much older you are than everyone you grew up with. You'd barely notice.
> SCENE: Pull back from the mirror to the window, and the street below full of strangers.
The bigger cost is the watching. A whole life spent ten seconds at a time, on strangers who'll never know your name.

## [06:15] Back to you

> MOOD: frozen
And then you notice something.

Somebody knows.
